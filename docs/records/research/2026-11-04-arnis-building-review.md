# Arnis building review for Mailwoman Earth

Issue #2928 tracks the work this review plans. Reviewed on 2026-11-04.

Arnis at `/home/lab/Projects/arnis`, commit d8e8b662 (`Merge pull request #1470 from louis-e/sports-venues`), Apache-2.0.
Mailwoman worktree at `/home/lab/Projects/mailwoman/.claude/worktrees/hrt`, branch `hrt-foundations`.

Every claim is marked **[read]** (seen in code or data), **[verified]** (checked against a public page on 2026-11-04), **[inferred]** or **[unknown]**. Paths without a repository prefix are relative to the Arnis root.

## 0. The short version

1. The Eiffel Tower isn't generated. It is a CC0 glTF from the 3D Model Repository (3dmr.eu, model 4) fetched at run time because OSM way 5013364 has `3dmr=4`, voxelized, and corrected by a hand-written override (yaw 44.3 degrees, scale 1.035, two-color palette). The way is also on a hard-coded skip list so no procedural tower is built under it. **[read, verified]**
2. Arnis has three landmark channels, all keyed on an OSM tag or id, none on name matching: `3dmr=<id>` (run-time glTF, 59 tagged objects in all of OSM), `wikidata=Q…` against a bundled index of 145 permissively licensed STL/GLB models (mostly statues and a few buildings), and five bundled schematics or code-built models (four Munich Olympic Park buildings and the Elizabeth Tower) pinned by Wikidata QID and lat/lon. **[read, verified]**
3. Ordinary buildings are good because of a long chain of deterministic, seeded, region-aware decisions: a 24-way category, a 5-way architectural era from `start_date`/heritage/architecture/material, a 23-way material region traced by hand-drawn lat/lon borders, a Koppen climate reweighting, a 128 m district cell that narrows each regional palette to 3-5 wall blocks and one dominant roof covering, a bay grammar fitted per straight wall run with corner piers, a ground floor chosen from street adjacency, and roof defaults by region, era, footprint and attachment. **[read]**
4. Overture fills OSM's missing heights and adds footprints OSM lacks. A Koppen 0.1-degree grid and the hand-drawn region borders cover the planet without per-city data. **[read]**
5. For Earth, the transferable parts are the decision tables (category, era, region, district narrowing, bay fitting, ground floor, roof defaults) and the landmark channel design (tag-keyed models with per-model overrides and a suppression footprint). The voxel-specific output code, Minecraft palettes, Mapillary photo facades and schematic props do not transfer. **[inferred]**

## 1. Landmarks

### 1.1 Three channels, resolved in order

`src/data_processing.rs:1256` runs the bundled-landmark prescan first, then `src/data_processing.rs:1346-1348` runs `Models3dPipeline::prescan` only when `args.use_3d` is true (default true, and `--no-3d` clears it, `src/args.rs:190-194`). Inside the pipeline, `src/models_3d/pipeline.rs:17-35` runs 3DMR before Wikidata, each receiving the set of OSM elements an earlier channel already claimed. Placement is in the order 3DMR and Wikidata models, planes, stadiums, then bundled landmarks "last because they clear their own footprint" (`src/data_processing.rs:1368-1398`). **[read]**

### 1.2 Channel A: `3dmr=<id>` run-time glTF (the Eiffel Tower path)

Recognition. `src/models_3d/three_dmr/placement.rs:92-152` (`prescan`) scans every element for a `3dmr` tag, parses it as a u64 model id, takes the element's centroid (`anchor_xz`, `:313-321`) as the anchor and its bounding box as the footprint, and reads OSM `direction=*` (numeric or cardinal, `parse_direction` `:387-421`) as yaw. It then suppresses every other element with `building` or `building:part` whose centroid lies in that footprint (`:155-173`). **[read]**

Geometry. `src/models_3d/three_dmr/client.rs:14-16, 103-150` fetches `https://3dmr.eu/api/info/{id}` and `https://3dmr.eu/api/model/{id}` (binary glTF, capped at 64 MB) with an on-disk cache under the OS cache dir. The `ModelInfo` struct reads `rotation`, `scale`, `translation` and `license` from the 3DMR metadata (`:17-37`). **[read]** For model 4 the live API returns title "Eiffel Tower", license 0 (the model page states CC0 1.0), lat 48.858093, lon 2.294694, description "A model of the Eiffel Tower taken from blendswap 67944", and no `rotation`/`scale`/`translation` fields, so the defaults (0, 1.0, zero) apply. **[verified]**

Placement, scale, orientation. `place_three_dmr_models` (`placement.rs:177-308`) composes a `WorldTransform` from the 3DMR intrinsic transform, the world scale, the OSM `direction` plus the world rotation, the anchor, and the lowest ground sample in the footprint (`lowest_ground_in_bbox`, `src/models_3d/mod.rs:14-45`). It voxelizes with `voxelize_glb_paint` (`src/models_3d/voxelize.rs:603`), which samples the glTF textures through a mip pyramid and maps each voxel's color to the nearest block (`closest_block`), with pure magenta `#FF00FF` as a glass sentinel (`voxelize.rs:10-19`). The model's lowest voxel is then snapped to the ground sample (`placement.rs:262-270`), trees inside the footprint are removed, and the voxels are stamped (`stamp_voxels`, `mod.rs:58-62`). **[read]**

The hand correction. `MODEL_OVERRIDES` (`placement.rs:46-67`) holds one entry: model 4, `yaw_deg: 44.3`, `scale: 1.035`, and a two-entry palette (`(77,51,36) -> BROWN_TERRACOTTA`, `(137,104,79) -> MUD_BRICKS`) with the comment "legs point N/E/S/W with the base edges at 44.3 degrees, the base is 125 m, and the iron is one brown rather than a texture patchwork". So the Eiffel Tower's orientation and size are not derived from data. They were measured against the real footprint and typed in. The paint path is replaced by `closest_of(o.palette, c)` (`:236-241`) so the tower is two browns rather than whatever the texture atlas samples to. **[read]**

Suppression of the procedural tower. Independently of the 3D pipeline, `src/element_processing/buildings.rs:3783-3784` has `SKIP_WAY_IDS = [5013364, 204068874, 32920861, 1352374225, 1486731987]` ("Never render as buildings: Eiffel Tower, London Eye, Utah Capitol, Starbase Pad 2 trench + mount ring") checked at `generate_buildings` (`:12277`). Way 5013364 is the Eiffel Tower (tags `3dmr=4`, `wikidata=Q243`, `height=330`, `building=tower`, `man_made=tower`, `building:colour=#706550`, `building:material=iron`, `building:shape=pyramidal`, `tower:construction=lattice`, no `direction`). **[verified]** Note the way has no `direction`, which is why the 44.3-degree yaw lives in code. **[inferred]**

Coverage. Taginfo reports 59 objects with a `3dmr` key in all of OSM (15 nodes, 41 ways, 3 relations, 35 distinct values) as of 2026-11-04. 3dmr.eu paginates eight pages of six models, so roughly 40-50 models are hosted. **[verified]** So this channel covers at most a few dozen landmarks worldwide, with one of them hand-corrected. **[inferred]**

### 1.3 Channel B: `wikidata=Q…` against a bundled index

Recognition. `src/models_3d/wikidata/placement.rs:98-124` scans every element with a `wikidata` tag and looks the QID up in a compiled-in index (`src/models_3d/wikidata/index.rs:7-8, 45-54`), which merges `assets/wikidata_3d_models.json` (auto-generated, 143 entries) with `assets/wikidata_3d_models_manual.json` (2 entries, manual wins). **[read]**

Index construction. `assets/refresh_wikidata_index.rs:178-186` runs a SPARQL query for items with a 3D model (P4896) ending in `.stl` and coordinates (P625), optionally with height (P2048, SI-normalized). It then asks the Commons `imageinfo` API for `LicenseShortName`, `LicenseUrl`, `Artist` and keeps only CC0, public domain and CC BY (`classify`, `:266-291`), rejecting ShareAlike, GFDL, NC and ND. A deny list (`:17-21`) drops Q9188 (Empire State Building, "wrong height_m (1500), generic block model") and Q41225 (Big Ben, built in code). The generated file records `license_policy: "permissive-only: CC0, Public Domain, CC BY (any version)"` and per-entry `license`, `license_url`, `artist`. License census of the 143 auto entries: CC BY 4.0 70, CC0 58, Public domain 13, CC BY 2.0 2. 67 of 143 have `height_m`. **[read]**

What's in it. Labels are mostly statues and monuments honoring people (Charlie Chaplin, Che Guevara, Mick O'Dwyer), a block of about 25 Venezuelan apartment towers ("Torre A de Karuay", ...), the Parthenon and Acropolis, a few German schools and halls. The two manual entries are the Statue of Liberty (186 m) and Christ the Redeemer (110 m), both CC BY 4.0 STL files by the Arnis author hosted at arnismc.com, with `palette_layers` giving banded colors by height fraction and a `yaw_deg` for the Redeemer. **[read]** I didn't find the Eiffel Tower, Tokyo Tower or Sydney Opera House in either file (Q243, Q216393, Q45178 are absent from the keys). **[read]**

Placement. The STL path (`voxelize_stl`, `placement.rs:~700-780`) derives an axis permutation and per-axis scale from the OSM footprint and height (`derive_fit`, `pick_up_axis`, as STL has no units or up axis), centers the model on the anchor, and voxelizes solid triangles, then colors voxels from either the curated `palette_layers` or a palette resolved from the element's `building:colour`/`building:material` or structure type (`resolve_palette`, `structure_type_palette`). Extents are capped at 225 m XZ and 600 m Y (`MAX_XZ_EXTENT_M`, `MAX_Y_EXTENT_M`). A curated `height_m` forces uniform scaling so the OSM footprint cannot squash the model. Terrain fit takes the median ground under structural columns and builds a stone foundation down to terrain (`footprint_ground`, `foundation_under`). **[read]**

### 1.4 Channel C: bundled landmarks (`src/landmarks.rs`)

`LANDMARKS` (`src/landmarks.rs:71-171`) has five entries: Olympiastadion, Olympiahalle, Olympia-Schwimmhalle and Olympiaturm in Munich as gzipped Sponge `.schem` files under `assets/structures/landmarks/` (5-133 KB each), and the Elizabeth Tower built in code (`src/structures/elizabeth_tower.rs`, 503 lines, a 17x17x~73 block model with dial, string courses and roof tiers typed as constants). Each entry pins a Wikidata QID plus a fallback OSM way id, a lat/lon anchor and the schematic XZ that lands there, a ground layer and offset, interior-marker blocks whose enclosed area is excavated, a suppression half-extent in meters and a reach. Anchors "are measured by matching each model against its real OSM footprint" (`:69`). Placement resamples the destination grid through the inverse rotate/scale so any world rotation stays hole-free (`place_one`, `:~590-720`), takes the median terrain over the suppressed area, and bridges foundations down to 64 blocks. **[read]**

Credits for these five: the `.schem` files are the Arnis project's own. The GUI credit list names third-party authors only for props such as cars, cranes and fountains, and lists none for the Olympic Park models. `src/gui/js/license.js` states that 3D models and facade photos are credited at run time from their metadata. **[read]** Whether the Olympic schematics have an external author is unknown. **[unknown]**

### 1.5 What makes landmarks "right", in one list

- The identity key is a stable id in the data (3DMR id, Wikidata QID, OSM way id), never a name. **[read]**
- The model replaces, rather than decorates, the procedural building: a footprint-based suppression set removes every `building`/`building:part` whose centroid falls inside, and the Eiffel way is also hard-skipped. **[read]**
- Scale comes from a curated height when one exists, else from OSM `height`, else from the footprint extent. Orientation comes from OSM `direction`, else a per-model override. **[read]**
- A per-model override table absorbs what the data gets wrong (yaw, scale, palette). **[read]**
- Terrain fit is a median over the footprint with a foundation under structural columns, so a slope doesn't tilt or float the model. **[read]**
- Tall models are reported to the terrain planner (`TallModel`, `src/elevation/peaks.rs:24-28`) so the world's height budget includes them. **[read]**

Coverage in numbers: 59 `3dmr`-tagged OSM objects, 145 Wikidata index entries (mostly statues), 5 bundled models. Beyond those, a landmark is an ordinary building with a `DetailTier::Landmark` decoration budget (`compute_detail_tier`, `buildings.rs:5604-5654`: footprint, height, category, `wikidata`, `tourism`, `heritage`, `historic`, `architecture` and a street face score into Minimal/Standard/Enhanced/Landmark). **[read]**

## 2. Ordinary buildings

Decision order in `generate_buildings` (`src/element_processing/buildings.rs:12250-13615`): era (`:12331-12337`), floor cycle (`:12325`), footprint flood-fill and holes, height (`:12537`), region and category (`:12601-12613`), facade plan from street and neighbor bitmaps (`:12645`), detail tier (`:12651`), climate and `RoofContext` with district seed (`:12660-12705`), `BuildingStyle::resolve` (walls, roof, chimney), window archetype (`:12811`), ground floor (`:13040`), podium plan (`:13093`), then the wall ring, frames, balconies, window life, cornices, quoins and roof. Every random draw is seeded from `group_seed` (the way id, or the shared id of a `building:part` group) via `element_rng`, so output is deterministic and parts of one building agree. **[read]**

### 2.1 Category (`BuildingCategory::from_element`, `:2026-2113`. `from_building_type`, `:2127-2192`)

24 variants (`:1989-2023`): Residential, House, Farm, Commercial, Office, Hotel, Industrial, Warehouse, School, Hospital, Religious, TallBuilding, GlassySkyscraper, GlassCornerSkyscraper, GridSkyscraper, ContemporarySkyscraper, ModernSkyscraper, MasonrySkyscraper, Historic, Tower, Garage, Shed, Greenhouse, Default. Order of tests: `man_made=tower` wins. Then if "tall" (more than 7 levels or over 28 m, set by `calculate_building_height`), a `StyleHint` from tags (`building_style_hint`, `src/osm_parser.rs:836-887`: glass/mirror material or glass roof -> Glass. Listed-and-not-modern, masonry material or cladding, ornate or brutalist architecture, or `start_date < 1945` -> Masonry. Modern architecture or concrete material -> Contemporary) picks the skyscraper family. A European block of 8-14 storeys with a ground-covering part and no tower proportions keeps its typed facade (`has_tower_proportions`, `:2212-2235`: more than 14 storeys or 50 m, or 40 m and 1.5x its longest side). A "true skyscraper" (120 m and 2x its longest side, `has_skyscraper_proportions` `:2249-2266`) draws one of five skyscraper looks by Knuth hash of the seed (18/12/15/25/15/15 percent split, `:2081-2091`). Otherwise TallBuilding. Below tall: religious by `building=*` or `amenity=place_of_worship`, then any `historic` key, then the `building` value table. Civic/public/government map to School. **[read]**

### 2.2 Era (`ArchEra`, `src/osm_parser.rs:895-905`. `building_arch_era_dated`, `:970-1023`)

Five values: Unknown, HistoricOrnate, TraditionalPreWar (before 1945), PostWarPanel (1945-1979), Contemporary (1980 on). Signals in priority: `building:architecture` (ornate styles -> HistoricOrnate, brutalist -> PostWarPanel, modern styles -> by year), listing (`historic`, `heritage`, `ref:nrhp`, `listed_status` not "no") -> HistoricOrnate unless dated post-war and not a place of worship, then the first 4-digit year in `start_date`/`construction_date`/`year_of_construction`, then material (panel -> PostWarPanel, masonry -> TraditionalPreWar, concrete/glass -> Contemporary), then masonry cladding. An outline's year is packed into bits 52-59 of its parts' shared seed so an undated part inherits it (`:694-717`). **[read]**

Era then controls almost everything: wall allow-lists per era (`era_allow_list`, `buildings.rs:5507-5572`), window archetype adjustments (`:4751-4790`), cornices (`:11520-11530`), quoin chance 0.5 vs 0.25 (`:10810-10816`), mansard odds, dormer odds (`:13947-13960`), weathering rate (`apply_condition_variation`, `:9177-9240`: 5 percent of blocks on HistoricOrnate, 2 percent TraditionalPreWar, 6 percent Historic/Religious categories). **[read]**

### 2.3 Height and levels (`calculate_building_height`, `:6399-6540`. `floor_cycle_for`, `:5977-6023`. `infer_building_height`, `:6046-6142`)

Floor cycle: 3 blocks for residential types and `building=yes`, 4 otherwise. When both `height` and `building:levels >= 2` exist, `floor((height - 2) / levels)` clamped to 3..5. Parts take 4 (3 in a housing group). Height: `building:levels` -> `levels * cycle + 2` (the +2 is a taller ground floor plus slab). The `height` tag overrides, minus `min_height` or a `min_level` offset, minus `roof:height` when a non-flat `roof:shape` is tagged, capped at 1000 m (`MAX_PLAUSIBLE_HEIGHT_M`, `:6148`). Relation levels come next, and inference applies otherwise. Inference is a per-type weighted storey table keyed on footprint area in square meters: houses 1/2/3 at 25/55/20 percent, apartments 3-6, offices by area band, retail 1-2, hospitals 4-7, churches as halls of 10/12/14 blocks, cathedrals 18-24, industrial halls 5/7/9, generic `yes` by area (under 40 m2 a utility box or one storey, 40-149 mostly two, 150-599 two to four, over 600 a 7-block hall 40 percent of the time). Tall flag set when levels > 7 or height > 28 m. **[read]**

Overture supplies heights for untagged OSM buildings before any of this runs (section 3). **[read]**

### 2.4 Roof shape (`BuildingStyle::resolve` roof block, `:2818-2925`. Helpers `:679-882`)

Priority: `roof:shape` tag (`parse_roof_type`, `:12195-12211`, twelve shapes), preset default for the category, parts with an explicit top are flat, enclosed buildings (90 percent of their ring walled by neighbors) are flat, then `european_default_roof` (`:719-822`), `regional_default_roof` (`:824-882`), then the auto-gable rule. European rule: urban types up to 26 m and 7 levels and 3000 m2 with a compact outline (fill/bbox >= 0.5) get a pitched roof at an era share (HistoricOrnate 0.95, TraditionalPreWar 0.88, PostWarPanel 0.40 or 0.10 commercial, Contemporary 0.25 or 0.05, Unknown attached 0.80, else 0.65), gabled when attached, hipped when free-standing, mansard 15 percent on ornate buildings over 400 m2 and 14 m. Regional rule: each `MaterialRegion` outside Europe and North America has a `RoofHabit` (house share, low-block share up to a height, hipped share, defined at `:208-249`, for example Japan 0.93/0.35/10 m/0.4, MiddleEast 0.04/0/0/0.3, Australasia 0.97/0.7/13 m/0.7), damped by hot desert or steppe climate (0.35, 0.7) except in Australasia. North American default: attached, over 13 m or over 300 m2 non-house is flat. `roof:levels` without a shape forces a pitched roof (mansard on pre-war attached North American rows and ornate European ones). Pyramidal when the footprint is near-square and small. Dormer chance by region and roof (`:13947-13960`: mansard 0.95 in Europe, European pre-war town block 0.65, North American house 0.12). **[read]**

Roof covering: 27 regional pools (`ROOF_POOLS`, `:8715-8744`) of weighted blocks (for example `BRITISH_ROOFS = slate dark 65, brick tile 25, orange 10`), spire/mansard/industrial pools, and a district share: 70 percent of a district's pitched roofs take the district's dominant covering, 40 percent outside Europe (`:8746-8753`). `roof_distinct_from_wall` (`:9029`) rejects a roof that clashes with the wall tone. **[read]**

### 2.5 Facade rhythm

Wall runs. `lay_out_facade_columns` (`:7617-7720`) walks the ring with Bresenham, merges consecutive segments whose headings differ by under about 14 degrees (`RUN_HEADING_COS = 0.97`, `:7579`) into one run, so a node where a neighbor's party wall meets the facade doesn't break the bays. A closed ring whose closing point falls mid-wall joins its last run to its first. **[read]**

Bays. `WindowArchetype` (`:4340-4364`) has ten rhythms with (window, pier, corner) widths in columns: Sash (1,1,1), Portrait (2,2,2), Spaced (1,2,2), Wide (3,2,2), Ribbon (4,1,1), Hooded (2,2,2), Piers (2,1,2), Strips (1,1,1), PairedPunched (3,2,2 with a stone mullion), DecoPiers (2,1,2). `BayLayout::plan` (`:4478-4524`) fits the largest whole number of bays the run allows between corner piers, gives the remainder to the two end piers, and widens the middle window or pier by one column for an odd remainder. A long row of single sashes drops a window rather than doubling one. The archetype is a weighted draw by category and region (`pick_window_archetype`, `:4642-4790`, for example Residential in Europe: Spaced 60, Portrait 32, Sash 4, Ribbon 2, Wide 2, and Industrial: Wide 40, Ribbon 50, Portrait 10), then adjusted by era (ornate Portrait becomes Hooded 60 percent, pre-war Ribbon/Wide become Portrait, post-war Sash/Spaced become Ribbon or Wide). Curtain-wall mullion spacing is every second column, every third 40 percent of the time or always for PostWarPanel (`curtain_rhythm`, `:4414-4426`). Department stores over 1500 m2 and 3 storeys get closed walls with 1-wide slits every 5 columns (`closed_retail_bays`, `:4428-4454`). **[read]**

Corners and towers. `ColumnFacade.corner` (`src/element_processing/building_facade.rs:~62-70`) marks columns within one block of a run end so glass never turns a corner. `TowerZoning` (`buildings.rs:3237-3335`) gives a pre-war masonry tower a stone base of 1-4 storeys (by floor count) and a stone capital of 1-3 storeys with a belt course. `plan_tower_crown` (`:14476-14540`) carves a setback wedding cake (North American pre-war masonry, 55 percent) or a plant screen (other towers, 50 percent) out of the mapped height. `plan_podium_tower` (`:14238-14289`) turns 40 percent of tall flat towers over 600 cells into a 2-3 storey podium with an inset tower. Corner quoins on 50 percent of pre-war buildings and 25 percent of others, always on a commercial/hotel/office/historic street corner (`generate_corner_quoins`, `:10781-10830`). Cornices and string courses on masonry eras and on all European rendered fabric (`generate_facade_cornices`, `:11498-11560`), deep cornice on zoned towers of 10 storeys. Balcony bands (`pick_balcony_band`, `:4163-4218`): towers stack balconies on every or alternating bays. Old European residential gets scattered singles, or the Paris "filant" band on the second and top storey (France 60 percent). Setbacks, chimneys, residential antennas, rooftop plant and water tanks by region (`src/element_processing/rooftops.rs:1-9`). **[read]**

### 2.6 Ground floors and entrances (`pick_ground_floor`, `:3868-3936`)

The choice is a Lobby for every skyscraper family, Plain when no wall faces a street, Shopfront when `shop=*` or a restaurant/cafe/bar/bank/pharmacy amenity, and otherwise a chance by category and corner: Commercial/Hotel 0.65 (0.85 on a corner), Office 0.40, Residential/Default with 3+ floors and attached or on a corner 0.60 North America / 0.45 Europe / 0.30 elsewhere, plus 0.15 on a corner. The shopfront is 3-wide display glass between 1-wide dark piers (`SHOP_RHYTHM`, `:4456-4460`) with a sign band and awnings. Street adjacency comes from `compute_facade_plan` (`building_facade.rs`), which classifies each wall segment as Street, Rear, Party or Open by sampling outward up to 8-24 blocks against the road bitmap and the footprint bitmap, and finds a street corner as two consecutive perpendicular street segments of at least 6 blocks. Entrances: mapped `entrance=*` nodes first, else a synthetic door on the street face, with a door style by category and wall and up to three doorstep steps on a slope. **[read]**

### 2.7 Color and material

Tag path first (`determine_wall_block_from_tags`, `:4931-4976`): `building:material` (or `building:facade:material`, `facade:material`) picks a material family, and a `building:colour` picks that family's nearest shade in Oklab (`FACADE_TONES`, 32 blocks each with a mean RGB and a family mask, `:4988-5061`, and `facade_tone_for_colour`, `:5125-5165`, hue-keeping with a max distance of 0.03). A color without a material maps to the nearest render tone, or for towers to stone/brick (pre-war) or concrete families. Color names follow the OSM wiki list (`src/colors.rs:55-105`). **[read]**

Otherwise the regional pool path (`determine_wall_block`, `:5229-5254`): `wall_typology` (`:5290-5348`) maps category, era, height and region to House/Traditional/Civic/Modern. `regional_wall_pool` (`:5350-5418`) picks one of 54 weighted pools per `MaterialRegion` x typology (for example `BRITISH_TRADITIONAL_WALLS`, `JAPAN_URBAN_WALLS`, `LATAM_HOUSE_WALLS`). `pick_district_wall` (`:5440-5489`) reweights by climate, keeps 3-5 blocks of the pool per 128 m district cell drawn by weight, and lets 12 percent of buildings stray to the whole pool (`DISTRICT_STRAY_CHANCE`, `:5433`). Rare blocks (nether brick, red terracotta) are accepted 20 percent of the time when drawn (`:5796-5812`). Trim stone for lintels and cornices is chosen to contrast with the wall (`trim_block_for_wall`, `:3938`). Window glass of a masonry tower is gray or black, never pale (`masonry_window_glass`, `:4793`). **[read]**

District seed: `district_seed(x, z, scale)` (`:628-640`) hashes the 128 m cell of the building group's bbox center with a fixed salt, so a street shares a few materials and one roof covering while the next quarter differs. Parts of one building take the district of the group center so a cell boundary never splits a building (`:12694-12702`). **[read]**

### 2.8 Regional variation

`MaterialRegion::at(lat, lon)` (`:208-215`) dispatches to three hand-traced classifiers: `in_north_america` (`:251-281`, Rio Grande border as a piecewise line, Canada by a 15-segment border including the Detroit and St Lawrence rivers, a Sunbelt box), `in_europe` (`:369-456`, Maghreb, Levant, Caucasus, East Europe by the Prut, British Isles by the Channel, Nordic, Mediterranean, Low Countries/North German coast, France west of the Rhine, Central and CentralEast), and `elsewhere` (`:460-595`, Australasia, Japan with the Ryukyus, Korea, Siberia and the steppe, Central Asia, Afghanistan by the Durand line, Middle East, South Asia under a Himalaya line, Southeast Asia under a Yunnan line, East Asia, Sub-Saharan Africa, Latin America and Southern Cone). 23 regions. The coarser `BuildingRegion` (NorthAmerica/Europe/Other, `:111-123`) drives roof habits, shop chances and window tables. **[read]** This is the one place Arnis hard-codes geography. It is a per-region table with lat/lon borders instead of a per-country branch. It fits the mailwoman convention of per-country data only if the table is moved to data. **[inferred]**

Climate: a bundled Koppen-Geiger 0.1-degree grid (`assets/climate/koppen.grid`, 142 KB, Beck et al. 2023, CC BY 4.0) sampled once at the bbox center (`src/climate.rs:12-13, 80`) into ten `Climate` classes. `climate_wall_weight` (`buildings.rs:5676-5758`) reweights wall blocks per class (hot desert: sandstone and white 4x, dark stone and timber 0, and boreal: spruce 5x on houses, falu red kept), `climate_gable_probability` (`:5785-5794`: 0.35 desert, 0.95 boreal, 0.90 else). Residential fabric adapts everywhere. Commercial fabric adapts only in hot climates (`climate_applies`, `:5661-5674`). **[read]**

### 2.9 What keeps it from looking repetitive

Everything above is a weighted draw rather than a lookup, seeded per building, and the narrowing happens at three scales: region (what's possible), district (3-5 materials and one roof covering), building (one draw). Added on top: per-block weathering by era and condition (`:9177-9240`), a `WallVariety` pass over plain wall blocks, roof membranes with patches and repairs on world coordinates (`rooftops.rs:21-60`), shutters, curtains, air conditioners and window boxes on dwellings (`generate_window_life`, `:11341`), awnings and signage on shopfronts, and the detail tier which spends more decoration on prominent buildings. Mapped `building:part` massing always wins over podium and crown synthesis. **[read]**

### 2.10 Two opt-in photo channels (not relevant to Earth's look but good to know)

`src/building_facades/` hangs 113 CC0 photographs (109 from Scouser on OpenGameArt, 5 from TextureCan, per `assets/building-facades/PROVENANCE.md`) on walls as Minecraft `item_display` entities, chosen by category and storey height with a scoring function and a spatial lattice so neighbors differ (`choose.rs:119-242`). It is off by default (`src/args.rs:357-358`). `src/mapillary/` (41 k lines) rebuilds real facades from Mapillary street panoramas into a 1 m class grid and an 8 px/m texture per wall. It needs a token and is on when a token is present. **[read via survey, flags verified]**

## 4. What transfers to Earth

### 4.1 Where Earth stands (mailwoman worktree, paths under `/home/lab/Projects/mailwoman/.claude/worktrees/hrt/`)

- Tile record (`packages/hrt/crates/hrt-profile/src/building_buffers.rs:12-34`): id, height, eave, base, wall color, roof color, 128 m district index (0-15), floors, roof shape (0 flat, 1 gabled, 2 hipped), rings, footprint, ridge, roof triangles. About 59 bytes for a flat rectangle uncompressed. The README measures about 34.8 gzipped bytes per building on a 4 km Osaka box (`packages/hrt/README.md:62-64`). No category, era, material, street flag, or model reference. **[read]**
- Profile inputs: Overture `height`, `num_floors`, `min_height`, `facade_color`, `roof_color`, `roof_shape`, `roof_height`, `has_parts` for the compact tiles. `class`, `roof_material`, `facade_material` are read only for the vector layer (`layers/buildings.rs`). Height falls back to `max(num_floors,1)*3 + 2` and is `0xFFFF` when neither exists. The decoder then draws 10 m (`decode.ts:28, 244`). Pitched roofs only on 4-vertex single rings with a known height (`building_buffers.rs:241-242`), with default rise `short/2 * 0.6`. **[read]**
- Color: the only Arnis port today is the 42-entry OSM color-name table (`layers/buildings.rs:250-294`, credited in a doc comment). Fallback is on the client: tagged facade, tagged roof, `DISTRICT_COLORS[district]` (16 off-white tints, `packages/cartographer/lib/base/theme.ts:163-180`), base. Tagged colors are mixed 50 percent toward `#f8f7f4` (`decode.ts:55, 247-250`). **[read]**
- Shader (`packages/earth/lib/scene/compact-buildings/layer.ts:167-184`): one window band per floor (`(top-base)/floors` or 3.2 m), a bay every 3 m (2 m on towers over 30 m), blank ground floor, blue-gray glass on towers, `fwidth` fade to average coverage. No corner, cornice, ground-floor or material treatment. The decoder leaves the roof shape byte unread (`decode.ts:207-211`). **[read]**
- Landmarks: two, Tokyo Tower and Tokyo Skytree, as a lon/lat/radius list in the profile (`layers/buildings.rs:35-38`, `is_modeled_landmark` `:88-118`) that drops matching footprints in both the OSM and Overture paths, mirrored in `packages/earth/lib/scene/Scene3D.tsx:71-78`, drawn as code-built lofted meshes (`packages/earth/lib/scene/meshes.ts:223-229, 285-291`) through deck.gl `SimpleMeshLayer`. No glTF landmark loader. GlTF is only inside 3D Tiles. `packages/structures/lib/international/sources.ts:133-145` already registers the Wikidata P4896 Commons catalog (STL, per-file license decision). **[read]**
- PLATEAU LoD2 (`packages/earth/lib/scene/city-models/style.ts:110-245`): per-feature base and height, district color from the same BigInt hash, roofs by normal, the same window grid at a fixed 3.2 m floor. **[read]**

### 4.3 Landmarks for Earth, concretely

What it would take to draw the Eiffel Tower, Tokyo Tower and the Sydney Opera House well:

1. A landmark manifest in `packages/structures` (the register already has the P4896 catalog row): per entry QID, OSM way id, model source (URL and format), license and artist, `height_m`, anchor lon/lat, `yaw_deg`, optional per-model scale and color override, suppression footprint (the OSM way polygon, or a radius). This is Arnis's `LANDMARKS` plus `MODEL_OVERRIDES` plus the Wikidata index merged into one data file. **[inferred]**
2. Models. Eiffel: 3DMR model 4 is CC0 glTF (verified), 330 m per OSM, needs the 44.3-degree yaw and 1.035 scale Arnis found. Tokyo Tower: the current code-built loft could stay or be replaced. I found no entry for Q216393 in Arnis's index. Sydney Opera House: no entry for Q45178 in the Arnis index and no 3DMR hit was checked. A glTF would have to be found or made, and the license decided per file. **[read for the index, unknown for 3DMR coverage of Tokyo Tower and the Opera House]**
3. Suppression in the profile: generalize `is_modeled_landmark` from a 2-entry radius list to the manifest's footprints (Arnis tests centroid-in-footprint for `building` and `building:part`). Tiles containing a landmark rebuild. The remaining tiles do not rebuild. **[inferred]**
4. Render path: a glTF loader in Earth (three.js already loads glTF inside 3D Tiles) placing the model at the anchor with yaw, scaled uniformly to `height_m`, base at terrain. Arnis's terrain rule (median ground under structural columns, foundation to terrain) translates to sampling the DEM over the footprint and taking the median. **[inferred]**
5. Scale of the catalog: Arnis's own ceiling is about 60 3DMR objects plus 145 Wikidata models plus 5 hand-built. A hand-curated list of 20-50 world landmarks with checked licenses is the realistic first tranche. The Wikidata P4896 sweep (CC0/CC BY only, STL mostly, height from P2048) is the automated second tranche, and statues dominate it. **[inferred from the index census]**

### 4.4 Costs in one place

- Tile bytes: category (1) + era/region/climate packed (1-2) + material and roof covering (1) + per-edge street bits (about 1) is roughly +4-5 bytes per building uncompressed on about 59, so under 10 percent. Gzipped growth is less because these fields repeat within a tile. **[inferred]**
- Shader: run-fitted bays, corner piers, ground floor and cornice are a few dozen more fragment instructions. The archetype and palette tables are small uniform arrays or a 1D texture. **[inferred]**
- Rebuild: any new record field means a planet rebuild of the compact tiles. I didn't find a planet-scale timing in the repository. The README gives only a 4 km Osaka box. **[unknown]**
- Landmarks need no planet rebuild, only a manifest plus per-landmark tile rebuilds and model assets. **[inferred]**
