# Training diagnosis and explainable decoding, v2

Written 2026-10-28, replacing "Measure the noise floor before blaming data" (`Training diagnosis and explainable decoding.md`) under the critique in `Training diagnosis and explainable decoding - Fable critique.md`. Each claim is marked **measured** (a number read from a project file or produced here, with the command), **inferred** (follows from measurements), **decided**, or **unknown**. Literature keeps the old bibliography's status: **V** means the abstract, paper or page was read in the research session, **U** means it was cited from memory and not re-read.

## Summary

1. The project has already attributed five training-process flaws with measured sizes. The old report's thesis that every attribution must wait for a noise-floor study is wrong. Two are label conventions in US register adapters (NPPES kept the unit inside the street in 25,359 of 197,963 test rows. Texas notaries labeled 1,855 PO boxes as streets), one is training exposure (the shipped model trained on 97 to 100 percent of every US comparison set and 80.8 percent of the held-out sample), one is a corpus-mix correlate (45,293 Who's On First admin rows ending in a country, 63 percent with a region before it, present only in the candidate's corpus) and one is a headline built on the wrong rows (the pooled +0.015 was the 9,460 gazetteer name strings. On 41,369 address rows the models tie).
2. Fixed: the NPPES adapter (`b3fb06863`, `splitTrailingUnit`), every single-line US register adapter (`b958a427b`), the headline. Open: no model has trained on the fixed rows. The thin-system cause is a correlate, untested. The arm decision was read on int8 with unequal overlays.
3. The ten frozen suites (26,743 decision rows no model trained on) have been analyzed here for the first time. 6,902 rows (25.8 percent) fail on all three models. 5,558 of those are in the venue suite, where the venue is read as a locality or a street, or the house number as a postcode. Four whole country cells (KZ, HK, IR, MO, 200 rows each) fail for every model, mostly because of our own rendering. 469 rows (1.8 percent) break on both candidate seeds. 250 of them are venues, and in 130 of those seed 42 reads the venue as a street when the next segment is `<Street> <number>`.
4. The decoder already exposes emissions, per-token margins, the priors that applied and the repairs that ran. What it cannot say is why an emission preferred a label, because the objective is token-factorized and has no phrase term. That limit is documented in `decoder-grammar.mdx`. The missing instruments are a per-span runner-up diff, a per-prior ledger and the registered constraint census, then the span-factorized path.
5. A 60,000-step run costs $9 to $12 at specification rates and $17 to $23 observed. A 90,000-step run about $26 to $35. A third seed is cheaper than an agent-night. The expensive resource is evaluation construction and labels. GPU time costs less.
6. Plan: six phases, four without a GPU, one GPU experiment of about $52 to $70, and decision rules that say whether to start screening runs, a CPU lane and locale modules. Another agent filled the CPU section.

Models. **Shipped** is `neural-weights-en-us` 10.7.0, trained on corpus `v0.7.10-gb-county`. **The candidate** (arm B) is `v830-base-c6-90k-mixed`, trained on corpus `v0.8.7-overlay` for 90,000 steps at batch 128, two seeds (42 and 43), staged at `/mnt/mw/models/arms/v830-base-c6-90k-mixed-s{42,43}/step-090000/full-cache`. No checkpoint was read).

Terms. A **seed** is the random seed of a training run. Two seeds of one recipe measure the noise of training itself. **Exact parse** means every expected span is predicted with the right tag and text. A row is a **both-seeds fix** when shipped gets it wrong and both candidate seeds get it right, a **both-seeds break** when the reverse holds. **Decision rows** are suite rows whose expected labels follow the address as written. **label-convention rows** follow a labeling choice of ours and are kept out of totals (`battery/suites.ts`). An **adapter** is the per-source code that turns a publisher's fields into labeled rows. **WOF** is Who's On First, the gazetteer whose place-name chains were sampled into the candidate's corpus. **Provenance** classes a held-out row by whether either model trained on it: `verified_unseen`, `unseen_by_candidate`, `known_overlap`, `unknown` (`HOLDOUT_AND_EVALUATION.mdx` §6.1).

### 1.3 Why no single flaw can be assigned to the candidate's lineage yet

Between the shipped recipe (`v7.10.0-gb-county-60k.yaml`) and arm C the diff is eight changes at once (measured, `fable-review.md` §4): corpus `v0.7.10` → `v0.8.7-overlay` (new split policy, WOF sampled by structure cell). Tokenizer `v0.9.0-multisplice` → `v0.10.0-c6` (vocabulary 73,143 → 48,000, `split_digits` on). WOF weights 2.0 → 15/7.5 (115/57.5 in arm B); `ban` 3.0 → 3.3. Two new invented sources; `val_rows` 4,096 → 24,576 (36,864 in B). Label-support floors; `augment_exclude_sources` for WOF. Iron rules 2 and 4 of `CONTRIBUTING_MODEL_WORK.mdx` both apply: every difference in the register or held-out comparisons is unattributable, since none can be assigned to one of them. Within the C6 lineage the arms are clean single-variable comparisons (A, B and C differ only in `init_from`, `max_steps`, the WOF weights, `min_draws` and `val_rows`), but B against C is confounded by the cosine schedule running over 90,000 rather than 60,000 steps. Several recipe constants were inherited without a measurement in this lineage: `class_weights`, `label_smoothing: 0.1`, `evidence_noise_prob: 0.15`, `locale_hint_drop_prob: 0.5`, `warmup_steps: 1000`, the cosine schedule.

## 2. What remains unexplained

Each line states the smallest test that distinguishes the remaining explanations.

- **The thin-system region misread (F4).** One explanation is that WOF chains teach `<name>, <country>` as a region. Test a thin-system evaluation set: score OSM rows from AL, AM, AE, BF, CI, CR and EC with a trailing country, plus the WOF `<locality>, <country>` chains from those countries, on shipped and the three arms. Count training rows by ending structure in both corpora (`model-overview-failures-and-regressions.md` §8 item 6, `fable-review.md` §6 step 5). Before scoring, explain the second-name-of-a-two-name-chain mechanism and decide how it applies to `Hamburg` and `Sevilla` (§2.11).
- **The designator split from its number (`STE` | `302`).** Speculation: the old NPPES and notaries labels taught units mostly from second-line `SUITE 200` forms. Test: arm B's recipe on `v0.8.8-nppes`, two seeds, every other setting unchanged (Phase 2 below).
- **The AU state-code merge (`CLYDE NORTH VIC`).** Not reproduced in title case with a comma. Speculation: the same two-name-chain mechanism, or an uppercase-locality effect. Test: `mwdev_minimal_pairs` ladders over case and punctuation of the same rows, then `gnaf` in full (3,795 rows. Shipped trained on all 825 in the sample).
- **The unit-word confusions (`H0101`, and the Norwegian street-type word after `Snorres` read as a unit).** No suite measures them: the OSM units suite renders bare units with no designator and scores about 10 percent for every model. Test: a units suite from a source that writes designators (the `v0.8.8-nppes` test split holds 52,806 unit rows with consistent labels. Norway needs Kartverket rows rendered with `H0101`).
- **The throughput loss (F9).** Test without a GPU: time the loader by itself over 2,000 batches locally with the `v0.9.0` and `v0.10.0-c6` tokenizers. Then, if the loader is not the cause, one bounded diagnostic run of 2,000 steps per tokenizer (`HOLDOUT_AND_EVALUATION.mdx` §8 lets a diagnostic run skip items 2 and 5).

## 4. The decoder as it is, and what is missing (Q3)

### 4.1 What a trace exposes today

The decoder solves `argmax Σₖ e(k, yₖ) + Σₖ M[yₖ₋₁][yₖ]` over BIO labels (`packages/neural/lib/viterbi.ts`, `viterbi()`), where the emission `e` is the model logit plus the soft priors and `M` is the structural mask only (0 for a legal transition, −∞ otherwise. The shipped `en-us` overlay ships no learned transitions). `NeuralParseTrace` (`packages/neural/lib/trace.ts`) records: the pieces. The five soft channels as fed (`anchor`, `gazetteer`, `country`, `streetType`, `localitySurface`) and any pins; `logits` before priors and `emissions` after them. One `TracePrior` record per prior kind (`queryShape`, `fst`, `anchorRegion`, `streetMorphology`, `spanProposer`, `venueHead`, `placetypePair`, `placetypeCensus`, `conventionsMask`) with `applied` true or false. The decoded `path`; `margins`, the decoded label's emission lead over the best other label in the same row (`pathMargins()`, attached at `classifier.ts:871`), negative when the mask or a prior chose a label the row ranks lower; `decode` (`viterbi` or `argmax`). And `repairs`, the post-decode passes that changed a label (`wordConsistency`, `postcodeRepair`, `unitRepair`, `jpMunicipality`, `krSubregion`, `spanBridge`) with before and after labels. After the argmax, `resolveProposalOverlaps` (`packages/core/lib/decoder/resolve-proposal-overlaps.ts`) keeps non-overlapping proposals greedily by descending confidence, then shorter span, then earlier start; `build-tree.ts` assembles the tree under `PARENT_OF`; `validate-tree.ts` reports illegal edges and stranded dependents. `decoder-grammar.mdx` §1 states that these passes are checks and local rewrites that cannot move the decode to a different global optimum. `mwdev_trace` returns the slim trace for up to 20 inputs and takes `config.channels` pins (`off` feeds zeros, the absence state the curriculum paints on a share of rows), which is already the channel-occlusion instrument the old report proposed to build.

Two traces run today through the dev engine (git head `9c7b50d19`, locale `en-US`, formatted input mode, the engine's default path rather than the suites' held-out inference rule, so they reproduce the shape of the failures and not the suite numbers):

- Candidate seed 42 on `BULEVARDI ZOGU I, NR. 1, TIRANE, Albania`: path locality `BULEVARDI ZOGU I`, locality `NR. 1`, region `TIRANE`, country `Albania`. Margins on the two pieces of `TIRANE`, 5.14 and 4.94: the emission row prefers `B-region` by five logits over the next label. Priors: only `fst` applied. The gazetteer and country channels fired only on `Albania`. No repair ran. The `NR`, `.` and `1` pieces sit at margins 0.42, 0.25 and 0.40, one perturbation from flipping. Shipped on the same input: street `BULEVARDI ZOGU I` at confidences 0.46 to 0.62, locality `TIRANE`, and the `1` piece at margin −0.52, a label the mask forced.
- Candidate seed 42 on `JYSK, Rruga Bylis 16, Tirana`: `B-street` on `JY` at margin 2.02, `I-street` on `SK` at 1.66. Priors `fst` only. The locale head reads DE at 0.99. Shipped: venue at margins 1.6 to 2.5.
- Both models on `Im Kirchfeld 17, Löcken, 52525 Waldfeucht` read `Löcken` as the dependent locality under this path. Shipped's second piece has margin −0.30, a transition-forced label, where the candidate's margins are 4.2 to 4.3.

So the trace answers "which stage decided": for the thin-system row the answer is the emission. A prior, a transition and a repair are each excluded. It cannot answer why the emission preferred `region`.

### 4.2 Why the trace stops there

`decoder-grammar.mdx` §1 proves it from the form: every term has arity at most two over adjacent tokens, and the arity-two terms are 0 or −∞. Two parses that differ only in the label of a delimiter-free phrase differ only through the emissions on those tokens. No term represents "this phrase is one constituent". Phrase cohesion, segment structure and cardinality exist only to the extent that the encoder's contextual emissions happen to encode them. §4 adds that registry evidence leaks to sub-spans (`Spain` inside `Port of Spain`), and §9.3 confirms by reading `buildFSTEmissionPriors` (`packages/neural/lib/fst-prior.ts:328`) that the FST prior applies bias at every accepting extension with no maximal-match preference. A ledger over this objective itemizes a choice as "the emission at token t favored region by 5.1", which is the operator's "cave wall" restated with a number (inferred, consistent with the trace above).

The objective the project means is span-factorized: `score(S, T, H | x) = Σ φ(i, j, t) + Σ ψ(h, d, r)`, with a toponym grammar `G_name` and registry evidence attached to whole spans (§2 to §4). The pieces exist: the span-boundary head is trained (`use_span_boundary_head: true`, weight 0.5 in the config) but `spanScores` is null in every trace because no `semi-crf-transitions.json` ships; `semi-markov-decode.ts` is the unshipped decode path; `classifier/loader.ts:133` names the "#727 phase-4c k-best rerank" as unavailable when the span grammar is undefined. The constraint predicates C4 (a boundary inside a delimiter-free phrase must be licensed) and C6 (no sub-span registry hit licenses a boundary inside a longer entry) are buildable as detectors today (§5).

### 4.3 What is missing, and what to add

Recommend only these, in order:

1. **A per-span local runner-up diff from the existing trace, with the decode unchanged.** For a chosen span, re-decode with that span forced to its best alternative label (a `transitionAdjustments` bonus of −∞ on the decoded labels of those pieces, an input `ViterbiInput` already accepts) and report, per piece, `logits` difference, `emissions − logits` (the total prior bias) and the path score difference. This is bookkeeping over values `traceParse` already computes. Cost about one agent-night. Runtime about 2× decode behind the trace flag. It changes no production parse, so it needs no release verdict.
2. **A per-prior ledger.** `TracePrior.applied` is a boolean. The magnitude each prior added per token and label is lost once they sum into `emissions`. Record each prior's delta matrix (or its non-zero entries) on the trace. Cost under one agent-night. No behavior change.
3. **The registered constraint-violation census** (`decoder-grammar.mdx` §9.1): run the C4 and C6 detectors over the 6,902 all-wrong rows and the 469 both-seeds-break rows. Count the rows each predicate explains and the rows it would flag falsely. This count decides whether to ship the span-factorized path by measuring the failures a phrase term could reach.
4. **Then the span-factorized decode**, opt-in behind a flag with tests that turn it on (`CONTRIBUTING_MODEL_WORK.mdx`, the opt-in half), graded on the suites, with its release requirement stated: a default-on decoder change ships only on a `pass` verdict, and the current specification registers no threshold, so the verdict is `insufficient_evidence` by construction until thresholds are registered (iron rule 5).

Not recommended: re-expressing the post-argmax passes as score terms (they cannot change the argmax, §1). A weighted-deduction rewrite before item 3 shows a phrase term reaches a large share of failures. Gradient attribution through the encoder, because no PyTorch checkpoint of either candidate seed exists locally (`find` over both arm directories for `.pt`, `.safetensors`, `.bin` or `optimizer*` returned zero files. Whether the Modal volume `/data/output-v830-base-c6-90k-mixed-s42/checkpoints` still holds one is unknown).

## 5. What the literature changes, placed under each decision

### 5.1 Seeds and significance (bears on Phases 0 and 3)

The project's instruments already reflect what the seed literature reports: the board's net count is a finding, never a verdict (`HOLDOUT_AND_EVALUATION.mdx` §7.1 item 4), and a corpus change is judged on two seeds against a control pair (§7.2). Reimers and Gurevych 2018 [V] measured up to 26 percent type-I error at p < 0.05 on CoNLL-2003 NER when _identical_ systems are compared by single scores, the failure mode the two-seed rule already guards against. The old report's "coin flip" heading dropped that condition. The literature supplies four tools the project lacks. First, a k-seed row rule: "changed" only when the two arms' majority labels differ and each arm agrees internally in at least k − 1 of k seeds, with other rows reported as seed-unstable (inference from Xie et al. 2021's negative-flip rate [V] and Yan et al. 2021's seed-against-seed flips [U]). A multiplicity correction across per-country tests, Holm or Benjamini-Hochberg, or Dror et al. 2017's replicability count of countries with improved scores [U]. Aggregate tests that treat seeds as the unit, almost stochastic order (Dror, Shlomov and Reichart 2019 [V]) or the Multi-Bootstrap over seeds and rows (Sellam et al. 2022 [V]). And a power analysis in the style of Card et al. 2020 [U] that takes the measured per-suite disagreement as input and shows whether 26,743 rows at k = 3 can detect a gain of the candidate's size. Dodge et al. 2020 [V] found initialization and data order contribute comparably to variance. That supports varying both when a third seed is bought. McCoy, Min and Linzen 2020 [V] found 100 seeds within 1.2 points in-distribution and 0 to 66 percent out of distribution. That is why the per-suite floors (0.0 to 23.6 percent) matter more than the pooled 8.9 percent. No source derives a seed count for a sequence labeler trained from scratch on tens of millions of draws. K is a measurement to make. A number quoted from a paper does not transfer.

Decision changed: a third seed of arm B ($26 to $35) is cheaper than the analysis that interprets it, so buy it before any per-country claim in a cell whose seed split equals the effect.

### 5.2 Label conventions (bears on Phase 0 and the suite relabel)

The project found four conventions by reading rows (NPPES, notaries, HRSA, the OSM `dependent_locality` for a bare place) and three in the suites (units, AU Photon, Moldovan postcodes). Klie, Webber and Gurevych [V] reimplemented 18 annotation-error-detection methods on 9 datasets and found that model-based methods find inconsistencies poorly while surface-form methods (variation n-grams, label entropy) find them well, with high precision and low recall (variation n-grams precision 0.60 at recall 0.01 on real CoNLL errors). A per-adapter convention is exactly an inconsistency: the same written form labeled by rule. The census the old report proposed stands: key the label distribution on (normalized form, left and right context), group by source, and rank the pairs that are pure within each source and differ across sources by row mass. It runs over the 126 sources of the held-out sample and the suites and costs no GPU. Rücker and Akbik 2023 [V] found 47 percent of a strong model's counted CoNLL-03 errors were correct predictions scored against wrong labels, 6 percent after correction. Northcutt, Athalye and Mueller 2021 [V] found model rankings flip when test-label noise rises by 6 percent. The project's NPPES case reversed the street comparison at 12.8 percent of rows relabeled (25,359 of 197,963), which is the same effect at larger size. Dataset cartography (Swayamdipta et al. 2020 [V]) can log per-row confidence statistics from one ordinary run and flag likely label errors. Phase 2 measures it at no extra cost if the trainer logs them on a stratified sample. Jain et al. 2022 [V] is the one adaptation of influence functions to sequence tagging, at segment level. It is the tool if Phase 2 leaves a residual designator-split error with no label explanation.

Decision changed: hand-label 200 to 300 disagreement rows against a written convention first (`fable-review.md` §6 step 3). The automated census then scales the decision rather than making it.

### 5.3 Training overlap (bears on the suites and stays off the critical path)

Lewis, Stenetorp and Riedel 2021 [V] found 60 to 70 percent of open-domain QA test answers in training and 63 points lower scores on items that could not be memorized. Elangovan, He and Verspoor 2021 [V] report the same leakage class in NER test sets. Magar and Schwartz 2022 [V] separate memorization from exploitation. The project's problem is the reverse of the one these papers audit: the published model trained on nearly everything, so a stricter overlap relation admits fewer rows. The fix the specification names (§6.4), an evaluation source outside both lineages, is what the suites are. Template-level near-duplicate matching (street, locality, postcode and order with the numbers abstracted) is a sound amendment to the overlap relation of §3 item 5 and to the suites' content join, and it is where the unchecked Overture, OpenAddresses and WOF exposure of suite rows would be measured. Attribution proceeds without it.

### 5.4 Error analysis and behavioral tests (bears on Phase 1)

The battery's seven causes already separate boundary errors (`street-extent`, `number-boundary`, `word-split`) from type errors (`venue`, `place-level`). Nejadgholi, Fraser and de Bruijn 2020 [V via search] motivate that split: 25 percent of a domain NER system's errors had the right label and an overlapping span, and experts accepted most of them. Errudite's rule (Wu et al. 2019 [U]) applies directly to section 3: a hypothesis such as "a leading comma segment reads as a street when the next segment ends in a number" must be checked on all rows with that shape, correct ones included, beyond the 130 that failed. Fu, Liu and Neubig 2020 [V] bucket entities by training frequency and label consistency. Both need a join against the training corpus, so the convention census of 5.2 serves them too. RockNER (Lin et al. 2021 [V]) and CheckList (Ribeiro et al. 2020 [U]) describe the invariance and directional tests that `mwdev_minimal_pairs` already runs (a ladder of near-identical inputs through one engine, reporting the first rung whose components change). The project tool exists, so the literature only supplies the test grid. Yassine et al. 2021 [V, full PDF] reordered 6,000 Korean addresses and accuracy fell to 28 percent: a multinational parser memorizes per-country order. That is the KZ venue-last and thin-system shape here. MESSY STREETS (Gaere and von Wangenheim 2026 [V]) shows non-canonical surface form by itself costs up to 25 recall points. That supports the suites' choice to render each country's own template order. libpostal's 99.45 percent full-parse accuracy [V] and deepparse's 99.6 percent token accuracy [V] are in-distribution figures on randomly held-out rows of their own synthetic distribution and are not comparable with each other or with the suites.

### 5.5 The decoder (bears on section 4)

Chang, Ratinov and Roth 2012 [V] give the template for explicit penalties, `w·φ(x,y) − Σ ρ_k d_k(x,y)`, which is what the C4 to C6 predicates become once their census is read. Sarawagi and Cohen 2004 [U] is the semi-Markov CRF the unshipped span path implements. Wang et al. 2016 [V] combine a CRF with a learned stochastic regular grammar for addresses and report 97 and 96 percent accuracy, and report no measure of explanation quality. It is the closest published precedent. Max-marginals (Smith 2011 [U]) give the local runner-up at one extra backward pass; `transitionAdjustments` in `viterbi.ts` is enough to force it without new code paths. Eisner 2016 [V] and Mensch and Blondel 2018 [V] make the decoded score differentiable, and Yin and Neubig 2022 [U] give the contrastive target (best minus runner-up). Both need a hookable PyTorch model. None exists locally, so they wait on a checkpoint policy. No paper measures whether an itemized margin helps people debug. The value is unmeasured, and item 1 of 4.3 is cheap enough to measure it on the 469 both-seeds-break rows.

### 5.6 Why channel-activation views are correlates (bears on 4.1 and the to-do)

Ravichander, Belinkov and Hovy 2021 [V] found probes detect properties above chance even when those properties were spread through the data as random noise. Belinkov 2022 [U] states the general point that a probe shows information is decodable. It does not show the model uses it. Elazar et al. 2021 [U] found probe accuracy does not predict behavioral importance once the property is removed. A gazetteer channel that fires beside a locality call may be correlated with the call rather than its cause, because retrieval hits and locality-shaped tokens co-occur in training (inference). The project's `channels: off` pin in `mwdev_trace` and `mwdev_minimal_pairs` is an intervention of the kind Geiger et al. 2023 [V] unify. What the pin lacks is a side-by-side margin delta. Item 2 of 4.3 gives one. Zhang and Nanda 2024 [V abstract] warn that the corruption scheme and the metric change the conclusion. The pin feeds the trained absence state rather than noise. That is the in-distribution choice. Hase et al. 2023 [V] found that where information can be restored from is not where an edit works. Makelov, Lange and Nanda 2023 [V] found subspace patches can activate dormant pathways. Both argue against layer-level patching or DAS as a first move at this model size. AxBench (Wu et al. 2025 [V]) found sparse autoencoders not competitive with difference-in-means. None of these is recommended before the decoder-level instruments.

### 5.7 Attribution and cheaper experiments (bears on Q4, section 6.1)

Bae et al. 2022 [V] show practical influence estimates approximate the proximal Bregman response function rather than leave-one-out retraining. A gradient attribution is a lead to test. TRAK (Park et al. 2023 [V]) matched retraining-based attribution at 17 A100-hours against 125 GPU-days on BERT/QNLI but underperformed a BM25 retrieval baseline on fact tracing, so a lexical or embedding nearest-neighbor baseline comes first. Per-source sums of projected gradients collapse storage to (sources × projection size). Simfluence (Guu et al. 2023 [V]) needs per-step probe losses logged in the run. DataDecide (Magnusson et al. 2025 [V]) found ranking at one small scale picked the 1B winner about 80 percent of the time at about 0.01 percent of compute, with continuous likelihood metrics. This is LM pretraining and a transfer claim for mailwoman. RegMix (Liu et al. 2025 [V]) and Data Mixing Laws (Ye et al. 2025 [V]) fit mixture effects from several small-scale runs. Llama 3's annealing test [V] and Blakeney et al. 2024 [V] value a data source by up-weighting it over the final 10 to 20 percent of training. Hägele et al. 2024 [V] show a constant-learning-rate trunk with cooldown branches scales like cosine and lets multiple branches share one trunk. Every arm in this lineage runs a cosine schedule with `warmup_steps: 1000`, so adopting cooldown branches is a recipe change that iron rule 4 requires to be proved on its own run. Model soups (Wortsman et al. 2022 [V]) and TIES (Yadav et al. 2023 [V]) apply only to branches of one parent, never to two from-scratch candidates.

### 5.8 CPU training and locale experts (bears on Q5 and Q6, sections 6.2 and 6.3)

libpostal [V] trained an averaged-perceptron CRF on over 1 billion examples in "a few days" on unstated hardware and reports 99.45 percent full-parse accuracy. CRFsuite's benchmark [V] trained CoNLL-2000 with the averaged perceptron in 48.1 s at 95.76 percent against 262 s at 95.99 percent for L-BFGS. Vowpal Wabbit's learning-to-search [V] trained CoNLL-03 NER in 69 to 123 s on a 2008 dual-core CPU. Lample et al. 2016 [V] and Ma and Hovy 2016 [V] put BiLSTM-CRF at 90.9 to 91.2 CoNLL-03 F1. SpaCy's hash-embedding CNN [V] runs at 10,014 words/s on CPU against 684 for its transformer at 4.3 OntoNotes points less. XtremeDistil (Mukherjee and Hassan Awadallah 2020 [V]) kept 95 percent of a teacher's NER F1 over 41 languages at 35× compression. No source reports CPU training throughput for any of these at scale. The measured figures are in the placeholder section below.

Conneau et al. 2020 [V] name the capacity trade-off of one shared model. Wang, Lipton and Tsvetkov 2020 [V] trace interference to language-specific parameters inside shared models. Rust et al. 2021 [U] attribute much of the monolingual gap to tokenizer coverage. That matches the Georgian and Armenian word splits here. Yassine et al. 2021 [V] found domain-adversarial training with country as the domain did not help on average and suggest address pattern and language as the granularity. X-MOD (Pfeiffer et al. 2022 [V]) adds languages after training with no measurable drop on existing ones because the shared weights are frozen. DEMix (Gururangan et al. 2022 [V]) adds and removes domain experts without retraining. MAD-X (Pfeiffer et al. 2020 [V]) swaps language adapters on a frozen encoder. Task-level MoE (Kudugunta et al. 2021 [V]) extracted per-task sub-networks that scored as well as the full mixture. Branch-Train-Merge (Li et al. 2022 [V]) found random data splits perform poorly, so expert keys must be meaningful. Token-level learned MoE (Shazeer et al. 2017 [U], Switch [U]) gives no locale alignment and no independent training, and NLLB [U] reports it overfits low-resource directions. The note's caveat, dropped from the old report, decides the question: modules cannot fix an error in the frozen trunk, and a trunk change invalidates every module.

## 6. The follow-up questions

### 6.1 Fewer full retrains (Q4)

At the measured costs the premise of the screening literature is weak here. A two-seed 90,000-step arm costs about $52 to $70 (derived). The #2857 experiment of fourteen runs cost $170.88. What is expensive is building the evaluation sets, auditing labels and reading rows, each about an agent-night, and those costs do not fall with proxies. Three things do reduce runs:

- Do not launch until the evaluation can grade (`HOLDOUT_AND_EVALUATION.mdx` P3, §8). Four runs, about 17 GPU-hours, were trained before anyone checked the evaluator could return `pass`. It could not. Phase 0 and 1 of the plan are that check for the next run.
- One variable per run, predicted in writing first (§8 item 5). Phase 2 is the one run with a measured, label-level cause.
- Find the throughput loss (F9). At 4.0 steps/s a 90,000-step run would take 6.3 h instead of 8.9 to 11.9. Every future run would then cost 30 to 45 percent less, larger than any proxy saving at this run count.

A shorter run has one measured lead in the project: the structure-board learning curve was flat after 30,000 steps for the one-stage arm (`model-overview.md`, sixth cost item, seed 42 only). Whether a 30,000-step single-seed screen predicts the 90,000-step two-seed outcome is a measurement to make on the A, B and C arms before adopting a screen protocol. A 30,000-step checkpoint of a 90,000-step cosine run differs from a 30,000-step run (the schedule confound, `fable-review.md` §2.5). The old report's proxies (1/10 width on 2 percent of rows) and anneal branches remain untested transfer claims and need a recipe change. They are not in the plan until the screen-accuracy measurement shows that a cheap arm ranks like a full one.

Controls, as the protocol defines them: the **self-control** stages the shipped weights through the candidate path and must differ on 0 rows. The **placebo** is a fine-tune of a base with no added data (about $1), used to attribute an additive fine-tune's regressions as candidate minus placebo. For a corpus change the control is the **control seed pair** on the previous corpus (§7.2). The "placebo anneal" and "random-source-out placebo" of the old report were new arms it proposed. The protocol defines neither as a control.

### 6.2 A CPU-trainable architecture (Q5)

What the literature supports: an averaged-perceptron CRF with hashed features is the one design shown to train at the billion-example scale on commodity hardware for addresses, and in-distribution address accuracy is saturated for both lineages, so a CPU CRF will sit close to the neural model on templated rows and behind it on unseen formats and scripts, where the suites show both lineages failing today. A distilled student loses about 3 to 5 percent relative in the cited NER figures and would need its own release verdict. It beats the shipped model only if its teacher does.

What a CPU lane is for in this project: a reproducible comparator on the suites. A lane for testing label and feature changes in hours (the convention census of 5.2 can be graded on it without a GPU). And locale patches trained in minutes on 100,000 to 1 M rows. It is not an accuracy replacement until the suites say otherwise. The measured time and cost on the lab host and on AWS are in the section "CPU training: measured time and cost" below, filled by the agent measuring them. The decision in Phase 5 reads that section.

### 6.3 Locale experts and mixtures (Q6)

Every regression measured in this project has a data-side cause. None is a shared-weight effect. The attributed causes are data-side: adapter labels (F1, F2), the WOF mix (F4), a rendering shipped memorized (F8), script coverage (F7, the Georgian splits), and the board's own label conventions (`Hamburg`, `Sevilla`). The decoder grammar, priors, lexicons and atlas features are shared, so a regression that lives in them survives any weight isolation. A pilot would start before its evidence. The test the old report proposed, "the regressions live in encoder weights", cannot select a pilot, because modules cannot fix the frozen trunk. If the regression is in the trunk, modules do not reach it, and if it is in a module, the trunk was not the cause.

What would change this: after Phases 0 to 2, a per-country loss that persists on `verified_unseen` address rows, fp32, identical overlays, in a country with ample training rows, where both seeds agree and the trace shows the choice in the emission with no prior applied. Then a pilot keyed by address system rather than ISO country (Yassine's granularity finding and BTM's split warning), on 2 or 3 systems, with X-MOD-style modules on a frozen trunk, compared against the joint model on those systems and on every other locale. There is no inventory of address systems to count the modules; `packages/codex/lib/address/system/conventions.ts` is where the systems the project already distinguishes are listed. Costs (derived): one joint base run about $26 to $35, then module jobs at a fraction of a run. The real cost is the release matrix of trunk × module × router versions, each module needing admitted held-out rows. Thin countries do not have them.
