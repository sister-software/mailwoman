# Dual-role places and hierarchy completion

Some places hold two admin roles. Berlin is a city and a federal state. Milano is a comune and the province named after it. Bristol is a city and a unitary authority. Utrecht is a city and a province. A census of our gazetteer found about **122 of these across nine countries**. Italy has 70 because nearly all of its provinces are named after their capital. Spain has 14, the UK 12, Japan 10, and Korea 7, and France, Germany, the Netherlands, and China each have a few.

These places cause a specific failure. In an international-order address such as `5 Hauptstraße, Berlin, Berlin 10115`, the city and the state are the same word. The parser often labels one `Berlin` as the region and drops the other. The resolved tree then has a region without a locality, and the resolver has no locality to place. On a 1,500-row Berlin eval, 955 rows resolved to no result.

## Completing the hierarchy

When the parser drops the locality of a dual-role place, the gazetteer still has it. The region and the locality are two linked records with the same name and a coincident centroid, so the resolver can recover the locality. With `hierarchyCompletion` enabled, the resolver checks the tree after the walk. If a region resolved and the tree has no locality node, the resolver looks the region up in a precomputed **coincident-roles relation** and synthesizes the missing locality from it.

The build derives the relation from the gazetteer's own structure, without a hardcoded list of city-states or a fixed distance threshold. It records a region and a locality as a pair when all three conditions hold:

- They share a name.
- The locality descends from the region in the `ancestors` table.
- Their centroids fall within a fraction of the region's bounding-box diagonal. This relative tolerance lets a large Italian province pair with a city tens of kilometers from its centroid while keeping a small city-state tight.

At runtime the resolver does an O(1) membership lookup, with no distance calculation and no constant to tune. Build the table once with `mailwoman-wof-build-coincident-roles`. The table ships in the admin gazetteer.

Each completion sets `metadata.relationship_type` to `city-state`, `capital-seat`, or `consolidated-county`, and marks the synthesized node with `metadata.resolver_synthesized = true`. The node has no span in the raw input, so a consumer can tell it apart from a parsed component.

## Picking the right city

A few admins map to more than one same-name locality, so completion has to choose one. The rule is [population first](./importance-vs-population.mdx). The principal city is the populous one, and it can sit farther from the admin centroid than a small same-name hamlet. For example, Niigata prefecture's centroid lies out at sea. The real Niigata city is 41 km from it, while a 0-population point is closer. Nearest centroid breaks a population tie. When two candidates tie on both, completion abstains, because a wrong locality is worse than a missing one.

Completion is on by default. A Berlin address that used to resolve to no result now comes back with its locality. Pass `hierarchyCompletion: false` to opt out, and resolution is byte-for-byte what it was before. Completion fires only for a dual-role region whose locality the parser dropped. It performs no work when the loaded gazetteer has no `coincident_roles` table.

## Attaching the lineage

A related option, `includeAncestors`, writes each resolved node's full containment chain onto `metadata.ancestors`, ordered nearest-first: county, region, country. Pelias and Nominatim do the same by default, returning each resolved place with its full admin hierarchy. The option reads the same gazetteer `ancestors` data that the coincident-roles relation is built from. It is off by default.

## Where this sits

Hierarchy completion runs in the resolve stage as a post-walk pass. A cleaner long-term home is the [reconcile stage](./joint-decoding-walkthrough.mdx). Its concordance scoring already ranks parses by how well their resolved places agree in the admin hierarchy, which is the signal that identifies the region Berlin and the locality Berlin as the same place. Until joint decoding is the default decode path, relation-driven completion recovers the dropped locality from the gazetteer even when the classifier did not surface it. A future concordance default could score against both the coincident-roles relation and the lineage attachment.
