We are nearing the goal of the last several weeks: **capability parity
with Mailwoman v0's rules parser**. The common tags (postcode, house\*number, street,
locality, region, US venue) are at usable parity. The campaign over the starved
long-tail tags has shipped `unit` (v4.1.0) and conditional `street_prefix`/`street_suffix`
(#462), and took `country` from 0 to 65 F1 on the hard homograph check, with perfect
precision and zero over-fire, across v0.9.10/v0.9.11. The remaining work is the hardest part
of the last 80%, and each move has to be the correct one.

This doc sets the direction for that stretch, based on the 2026-06-09 design
review (the model-first reversal, the synonymy/homonymy framing, and the gazetteer-
anchor discussion). Three principles now govern the endgame, and a concrete move
order follows from them.

## Status (2026-06-10): country banked

Move 1 is **done**. The general gazetteer anchor took `country` from 0 (starved in
v4.1.0) to **83.3 F1** on the hard homograph check (recall 74.1, precision 95.2,
**over-fire 0**), and lifted the guardrail with it (region US +11.7, locality +14.9).
Progression: v0.9.10 balanced extract 57.9 → v0.9.11 +class-weight 65.0 → v0.9.12
+gazetteer anchor 83.3. The one cost was US postcode −3.7. The region clue ("CA",
"GA") next to the US ZIP strengthens `B-region` and weakens the
`B-region → B-postcode` CRF transition (US-only; FR's postcode precedes the
locality, so it never sees this). The fix is **train-time channel choreography**:
zero the gazetteer clue on tokens adjacent to a postcode-anchor hit, at _both_ train
and inference time. Both are required because the harm is baked into the weights, so an
inference-only patch cannot undo it. Operator decision: **accept v0.9.12/13 as the
country change and carry the choreography into the consolidation retrain** (move 4),
where the dip is expected to disappear, without a standalone fix-retrain. The choreography ships
default-off and byte-stable in PR #468.

## The three governing principles

**1. Model-first, lexicon-as-evidence.** The central correction from that review is that no
deterministic override ever replaces the model's output. _Training_ covers closed-vocab tags
(balanced extracts with homograph contrast). The gazetteer enters as a
**soft input-layer clue**, a per-token candidate-tag set the model conditions on,
and never as a post-parse verdict. Measured grounding: the balanced country extract took
`country` from 0 to alive at P=100 / over-fire=0 (data alone), and the remaining
recall gap is exactly the long-tail the clue channel exists for. See
[closed-vocab fields: model-first](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/main/docs/engineering/reference/closed-vocab-fields-model-first.mdx)
and the new [gazetteer-anchor rung](../site-2026-08/understanding/our-approach/the-knowledge-ladder.mdx)
in the knowledge ladder.

**2. Canonicalize for synonymy, disambiguate by stage, never drop a span.** The parser
resolves _tag_ ambiguity from in-string context; the resolver late-binds the
_referent_ with geographic context. Every span survives the parse, either classified or
explicitly `unknown`, and each carries a canonical value. The lossless typed decomposition
is a deliverable in its own right (record linkage, dataset matching) rather than a
by-product of geocoding. See
[synonymy and homonymy](../site-2026-08/concepts/synonymy-and-homonymy.mdx).

**3. Soft priors rather than hard masks.** Positional masks and user hints (focus country,
focus point) re-enter the system as _features_ that the model or resolver can overrule. Rules systems
depend on these signals as hard constraints, and this version keeps them soft.

## The move order

1. ~~**Finish country (step 2b): the gazetteer anchor, built general.**~~ **DONE
   (2026-06-10).** A per-token **candidate-tag-set** feature channel (multi-hot over
   `country, region, po_box, cedex, …`), sourced from codex, uses the same
   input-layer mechanics as the postcode anchor and phrase priors. Country was the
   first consumer, because its long-tail recall (Eswatini, Timor-Leste, multi-word names)
   was the measured gap. Region/locality/street membership can be added later at little cost.
   The check passed: country 0 → 83.3 F1, precision/over-fire intact, guardrail lifted. See
   the status section above. po_box/cedex use the same channel at no extra cost.
2. **Promote the affix change via the multi-locale reroll (#466 step 1).** v0.9.8's
   only defect is FR-postcode dilution from a US-only extract, and the multi-locale country
   extract already demonstrated the recovery. Reroll affix multi-locale and pass its own
   check, without new annotation.
3. **po_box + cedex coverage extracts, following the unit playbook.** These tags are closed-vocab
   but homograph-free, so they need balanced extracts. Their matcher accuracy is
   already measured at 100% as feature-sources/eval material. Prove each one separately.
4. **Consolidate (#466): one model carrying all banked changes.** Weight-merge or a
   single multi-tag run at a larger step budget. The cumulative-dilution result showed that
   stacking extracts into a fixed 20k budget fails. This run also **carries the gazetteer
   choreography** (PR #468), and the v0.9.12 US-postcode dip is expected to disappear
   in it. It gets a full per-tag regression check and becomes the v0-parity release.
5. **Then the pivot: from parity to the things v0 cannot do.** The lossless
   decomposition (typed `unknown` spans surviving projection — the small decoder
   change), canonical values on every span, and record-linkage as a named
   capability. Parity is the prerequisite for this work rather than the final goal.

## What this retires

- The `ClosedVocabTagger` post-parse override (#464's original scope), rejected on
  principle and on evidence. The issue is rescoped to the anchor.
- Country/po_box/cedex as "deterministic" changes in the parity scorecard's taxonomy —
  corrected to model-first; the deterministic probes stand as matcher-accuracy
  evidence and live on as feature-sources.
- Per-tag, one-off anchor builds — superseded by the general candidate-tag-set
  channel (one mechanism, several tags).
