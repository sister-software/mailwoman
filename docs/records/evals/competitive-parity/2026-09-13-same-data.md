# Same-data controlled resolver benchmark (2026-09-13)

Every arm reads one frozen candidate fixture, so a measured difference is attributable to selection
rather than to retrieval, index vintage, or data footprint. The registered claim was:

> Given the same frozen candidate fixture, Mailwoman selects the correct admin candidate more often
> and makes fewer wrong-area selections than the registered baseline resolver.

**The claim does not hold as registered.** Mailwoman exceeds the baseline's pooled selection accuracy
by 24.4 points with an exact McNemar p of 3.488e-17, clearing both the registered 8-point margin and
the significance condition. It fails the secondary no-regression condition: in the withheld-gold
stratum its false-selection rate is 75.0% (75/100) against the baseline's 57.0% (57/100). Mailwoman
selects better when the answer is present — 69.7% against 45.3% pooled — and manufactures a place on
75 of 100 rows where no correct candidate exists, against the baseline's 57.

## What this measures, and what it does not

Two properties of the shipped code decide the design, both read from source:

1. `resolveTree` (`packages/resolver/lib/resolve/index.ts`) picks a place **per node**, up to
   `maxLookups` backend calls per row — not one selection per query.
2. Rooftop coordinates come from `AddressPointLookup.find()` / `InterpolationLookup.find()` /
   `StreetCentroidLookup.find()`, each returning one hit or null. There is no candidate set at that
   tier.

So `ResolverBackend.findPlace` is the only interface in the resolution ladder that returns a
candidate set, and the benchmark measures **admin candidate selection** there. It says nothing about
global coverage, index quality, retrieval, rooftop accuracy, or performance against any third-party
geocoder. Those belong to the end-to-end record at
[`2026-08-18-open-truth-three-arm.md`](./2026-08-18-open-truth-three-arm.md), which this work does not
alter.

**Three of the five strata are synthetic constructions.** The reordered rendering, the contradictory
postcode, and the withheld gold are mechanical transforms applied without inspecting rows. They
measure the refusal and recovery rules. They carry no claim about the rate at which those conditions
occur in real input; this panel does not measure that rate.

**The parser is outside the scored unit.** The fixture freezes the parse from model version 9.1.0 and
both resolver arms walk that tree. A difference here is a resolution difference.

## Panel and provenance

Gold entities come from GeoNames `cities15000.txt` (CC-BY-4.0, attribution GeoNames, 33,871 rows,
sha256 `9bd8b53f96f0e8c69c2ca6485739b82e45599422d8423335962cde400a1ca912`), joined to the WOF ids the
backend answers with through the `gn:id` concordance in `admin-global-priority.db`. The postcodes in
the contradictory stratum come from GeoNames `allCountries-postal.txt` under the same terms.

The identity join is guarded, and the guard is where most exclusions come from:

| outcome of the identity join                                  | rows of 33,871 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------: |
| no `gn:id` concordance                                        |         20,406 |
| concorded, but no locality/localadmin row                     |          1,152 |
| city-tier row whose folded name disagrees                     |          1,492 |
| city-tier rows split across countries                         |             11 |
| city-tier row further than 25 km from the register coordinate |             72 |
| **coherent gold identity set**                                |     **10,738** |

Grading a locality selection against a region's id would mark a correct answer wrong, which is what
the placetype half of the guard prevents. The gold is a SET rather than an id because 21 of the 10,738
coherent sets hold more than one distinct city-tier WOF id — the gazetteer carries that place twice.
Counted over concordance ROWS rather than distinct ids that figure reads 4,304, because the table
writes the same link more than once (2,057,196 rows over 1,775,438 distinct pairs).

yield. The stratum clears the registered floor of 50, so its rows enter the pooled decision; pooling
strata of unequal size weights them unequally, and each stratum's n is printed beside every pooled
figure.

The ablation turns off six default-ON options — `adminCoherence`, `postcodeConsistency`,

fame ranking, the country prior and the bare-toponym race cannot be switched off without a fork, and
they stay on in both resolver arms.

## What the result supports

Inside the claim boundary, one sentence is supported and one is refused.

Supported: given the same frozen candidate evidence, Mailwoman selects the correct admin candidate at
69.7% against a deterministic exact-agreement baseline's 45.3% pooled — 24.4 points, interval 19.0 to
29.7 — and it leads in every stratum where selection accuracy is defined. Its wrong-area rate is also
lower pooled, 19.2% against 30.4%.

Refused: the registered claim as a whole, because of the withheld-gold stratum. When no correct
candidate exists, Mailwoman answers anyway on 75 of 100 rows against the baseline's 57. The ablation
sits with the baseline at 57, which places the behaviour in the ranking rather than in the six
coherence and recovery passes.

## Next

The withheld-gold result is the finding worth acting on: an abstention threshold the resolver does not
currently have. `ResolveOpts.minWinningScore` exists and defaults to 0, which is the knob nearest to
it; whether raising it recovers abstention without costing selection accuracy is a measurement this
fixture can answer without a new panel, because both quantities are already scored on the same rows.
