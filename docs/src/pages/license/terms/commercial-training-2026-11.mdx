# Mailwoman Commercial Training Agreement

**Version `commercial-training-2026-11`.** These are the standard terms of a Mailwoman commercial training
agreement. They bind only when Licensor and Licensee sign an order form that incorporates them by this version. A
training agreement is negotiated in writing and is never sold through checkout. The version is written into every
training grant issued under the agreement. This page is not edited after publication; a later version is published
at its own address.

The [platform agreement](/license/terms/commercial-2026-11) excludes the training stack in its section 2a. These terms
grant it.

## 1. Parties and order form

Licensor: Nirrius LLC (d/b/a Sister Software)
Contact: hello@sister.software

Licensee: The legal entity named in the order form.

The order form states the term, the fees, the training environment (section 3), whether Licensee holds a
post-term deployment grant (section 6), and any limit on the models or model families the agreement covers. Where
the order form and these terms conflict, the order form governs.

## 2. Covered training operations

During the term, Licensor grants Licensee a worldwide, non-exclusive, non-transferable, non-sublicensable license to
use the Mailwoman training stack without AGPL copyleft obligations to:

- Train a model from scratch
- Fine-tune or continue training a pretrained model Licensor publishes for Mailwoman, or a model Licensee trained
  under this agreement
- Retry, resume, or branch a training run from one of its checkpoints
- Export, quantize, and split the resulting models with the Mailwoman export tools

The training stack is the trainer (`packages/trainer`), the training launcher and its managed training service, the
training configurations and corpus recipes, and the model export, quantization and embedding tools. A model
produced by these operations during the term is a "Trained Model".

The grant covers only the code and model artifacts that Licensor has authority to license, as section 2 of the
platform agreement describes. It does not extend to the corpus sources, gazetteers, or other third-party inputs a
training run reads. Their terms continue to apply to training on them and to any model trained on them, and
Licensee is responsible for holding the rights a run's inputs require. A run's corpus must pass the corpus admission
checks the training stack applies on either license branch.

## 3. Training grants

Licensor issues Licensee a signed training grant, a credential with a stable grant ID, this agreement's version, an
expiry date, and the permissions it carries. The commercial trainer verifies a grant before every start, retry and
resume, and refuses a run its grant doesn't admit. A grant runs for at most one calendar quarter, and Licensor renews
it under the same grant ID while the agreement is in force.

The grant is a credential. Licensee keeps it confidential, stores it only in the secret store of its training
environment, and doesn't place it in a log, a configuration file, a model artifact, or any file Licensee
distributes.

The order form names one training environment:

- **Managed.** Licensor runs the training and export in its managed training service. Licensor signs each Trained
  Model's provenance manifest, which records the run that produced the model and the grant that admitted it, and binds
  the model's bytes.
- **Licensee-operated.** Licensee runs the commercial trainer in its own environment. On request, Licensor signs a
  manifest Licensee submits, after checking that the submitted files match the submitted digests. That signature
  states only what Licensor verified, and the training record in the manifest remains Licensee's own statement.

## 4. Use and redistribution of Trained Models

During the term, Licensee may, without AGPL copyleft obligations for the material Licensor licenses:

- Use Trained Models internally and in Licensee's products and hosted services
- Distribute Trained Models to Licensee's customers in binary form, as part of a Licensee product that does more than
  serve the model

Licensee may not distribute a Trained Model as a standalone model or model library, offer it to third parties to train
or fine-tune further, or sublicense the training stack.

A Trained Model keeps Licensor's copyright and provenance notices. Each published Trained Model's provenance
manifest records the grant ID under which it was trained.

## 6. Term, expiry and post-term rights

The agreement runs for the term the order form states and renews only by a signed renewal.

When the agreement expires without renewal:

- The training grant stops admitting new runs, retries, resumes and exports at the end of its expiry date in UTC. A
  training process admitted before that time may run to completion.
- The rights in section 4 end, unless the order form grants a post-term deployment grant. A post-term deployment
  grant is a signed grant with the same grant ID that permits deployment; it covers continued use and distribution,
  as section 4 describes, of the Trained Models whose provenance records that grant ID, for the period the order form
  states.
- Provenance records remain records of how a Trained Model was produced. A record is not a grant, and it permits no
  use after the rights it records have ended.

When Licensor terminates the agreement for Licensee's material breach, every right under it ends at termination,
including any post-term deployment grant.

After the rights under this agreement end, a Trained Model that qualifies as a covered work under AGPL-3.0-only may be
used only under AGPL-3.0-only.
