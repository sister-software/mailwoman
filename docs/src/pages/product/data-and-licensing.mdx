# What licenses apply to Mailwoman and its data?

Published `mailwoman@11.0.0` has separate rights records for its software, model training inputs, and geographic databases. The bundle registry records source terms, while an installed artifact may need further source-level review. The software and bundle records below were checked on 10 November 2026. The OpenStreetMap guidance was checked again on 25 November 2026. A recorded license expression doesn't decide every obligation for a particular redistribution or hosted use.

## Software and model artifacts

The published `mailwoman@11.0.0`, `@mailwoman/neural@11.0.0`, and `@mailwoman/neural-weights-en-us@11.0.0` manifests declare `AGPL-3.0-only OR LicenseRef-Commercial`. The repository's [license text](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/v11.0.0/LICENSE.md) offers AGPL-3.0-only use or a separate commercial agreement. The AGPL text includes source-disclosure conditions for covered network use. The repository's [commercial license template](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/v11.0.0/COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md) grants no rights until a specific agreement is executed. Read the [current license page](/license) and the applicable executed agreement for your use.

The commercial template limits its grant to code and model artifacts the licensor has authority to license. It states that third-party components and separately downloaded reference databases keep their upstream terms. The published en-US weights package includes [LICENSE.md](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/v11.0.0/packages/neural-weights-en-us/LICENSE.md) and [PROVENANCE.json](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/v11.0.0/packages/neural-weights-en-us/PROVENANCE.json). Its provenance record identifies OpenStreetMap-derived training inputs and their attribution. The presence of a training notice doesn't, by itself, establish the license of every later prediction or customer database. That application-specific interpretation needs owner or legal review.

The site publishes versioned [SPDX 2.3](/sbom/mailwoman-11.2.0.spdx.json) and [CycloneDX](/sbom/mailwoman-11.2.0.cdx.json) software bills of materials for `mailwoman@11.2.0`, plus a [CycloneDX data bill of materials](/sbom/mailwoman-data-11.2.0.cdx.json). The data bill of materials lists each source inside the US address layer with its publisher's decision, terms and obligations. Check the version and component identity in each file before using it for an inventory. The [packages and runtime reference](/docs/reference/packages-and-runtime) identifies the directly installed packages for the checked library path.

## Data bundles and their recorded terms

The bundle terms below describe recorded permissions and unresolved questions. A data holder may grant separate permission for model training, geographic retrieval, internal validation, and disclosure of records or conclusions. The published bundle registry doesn't itself grant all four purposes for another dataset. [What an address result can establish](/docs/explanation/trust-and-evidence) explains the distinction, and [review a geocoding result's evidence](/docs/guides/review-geocode-evidence) gives a record-specific review path. A rights owner must approve a proposed use against the exact source and artifact.

## OpenStreetMap attribution

The published en-US weights package records OpenStreetMap-derived training inputs in its [provenance file](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/v11.0.0/packages/neural-weights-en-us/PROVENANCE.json). That training notice describes the model's inputs. An operator may also build and install a separate OpenStreetMap address-point database for geographic lookup. Its layer manifest identifies that database's source and credit. [Review a geocoding result's evidence](/docs/guides/review-geocode-evidence) shows how to inspect both records for an installation.

OpenStreetMap's [copyright page](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) calls for credit to OpenStreetMap contributors and a notice that the data is available under the Open Database License. The OpenStreetMap Foundation's [attribution guideline](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Attribution_Guidelines) addresses public geocoders, applications using them, and model documentation separately. A public service using an OpenStreetMap lookup database needs a credit readers can find. A source stamp in one API response is insufficient as the service's only credit. A notice for that use can read:

> Address data © [OpenStreetMap contributors](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). OpenStreetMap data is available under the [Open Database License 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/).

The [geocoding guideline](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Community_Guidelines/Geocoding_-_Guideline) distinguishes individual results from a database assembled through substantial or systematic extraction. The exact database, public use, and redistribution determine which additional obligations need review. The published bundle registry and a model's training notice cannot decide those questions for an operator's installation. Send the artifact and intended use to the data owner or legal reviewer before stating that its attribution and database obligations are complete.

## Read the source publishers' terms

The following publisher pages were checked on 10 November 2026. They describe their own data and may describe a later release than the paths above.

- [OpenStreetMap's copyright page](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) identifies ODbL data. It requires credit to OpenStreetMap contributors and a clear notice that the data is available under ODbL when the data is used. It also describes the same-license condition for altered or built-upon data. The `candidate` bundle records Northern Ireland postcode rows derived from OpenStreetMap. A separately built OpenStreetMap address-point database is another ODbL data path. Keep the applicable data artifact and its notice identifiable, and obtain owner or legal review of a proposed redistribution.
- [Who's On First's license page](https://www.whosonfirst.org/docs/licenses/) requires a link to its license and lists other sources that may have their own terms. The `candidate` bundle uses Who's On First place data. The registry hasn't traced each candidate name back to an upstream source.
- [GeoNames' publication page](https://www.geonames.org/about.html) describes its downloadable data as Creative Commons Attribution. The published registry records CC-BY-4.0 for the `candidate` bundle's GeoNames contribution. [Creative Commons' CC BY 4.0 terms](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) describe credit, license-link, and change-marking conditions when material is shared.
- [Overture's attribution page](https://docs.overturemaps.org/attribution/) lists terms by theme and source. The published registry assigns ODbL to Divisions in `candidate` and CDLA-Permissive-2.0 to its pinned `poi` build. Overture's current Places section lists source-level CDLA and Apache terms. Review the pinned artifact's source records before treating either summary as its complete notice.
- [Base Adresse Nationale's data page](https://adresse.data.gouv.fr/contenu-de-la-ban) states that its data is available under an open license. The published `fr` registry entry identifies the elected Licence Ouverte 2.0 path. Review the [recorded license text](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/v11.0.0/packages/corpus/data/licenses/licence-ouverte-2.0.md) and the artifact's source date for attribution.

The `us` bundle also draws on the United States Department of Transportation's National Address Database and Census TIGER data. The registry records that source bodies and permitted uses need further review, including its warning about mailing-list use. The [Department of Transportation's database page](https://www.transportation.gov/gis/national-address-database) identifies the database and its contributing levels of government. A public-domain statement about federal work doesn't resolve the terms of each state or local contribution in the bundle.

For a particular installation, inspect the selected bundle and its source records before publishing an attribution notice or redistributing data. The rights owner or legal reviewer needs to resolve the `candidate`, `fr`, and `us` questions above against the exact artifact and intended use. [Limits and release status](/product/limits-and-status) distinguishes a bundle listed in the registry from one installed in an environment.
