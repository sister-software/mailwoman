# Benchmark evidence and status

The published `mailwoman@11.0.0` package contains an en-US model identified as version `10.8.0`. The inspected [evaluation ledger](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/v11.0.0/evals/scores-by-version.json) has a promotion-battery entry for that model, but its reported percentages do not include the row denominators needed for a customer benchmark. The [release requirements, version 7](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/v11.0.0/packages/mailwoman/tools/eval-harness/release/specs/release-requirements.v7.json) leave the dataset, candidate, and evaluation identities unset. These records do not establish a release-matched coordinate-accuracy figure, parse-accuracy rate, throughput result, or `pass` release verdict for 11.0.0. [How data and evaluation support quality claims](/docs/explanation/data-and-model-quality) explains that release evidence path.

The historical [Belgium result](/benchmarks/be-results.json) illustrates the difference between a result and a correct place: the `base` arm returned a coordinate on 27 of 30 queries, while 24 of those same 30 queries landed inside the recorded Belgium bounding box. The `country-pinned` arm placed 30 of 30 inside that box. Every answered row in these arms used an administrative tier. A bounding-box hit does not verify a street or building coordinate.

The [open-truth scorer](/benchmarks/open-truth-three-arm.mjs) was run offline against its committed result rows for this index. At 1 km, its historical Mailwoman arm had 258 of 297 rows inside the threshold, compared with 273 of 297 for Pelias and 255 of 297 for Photon. Those counts use the same 297 published rows per arm. They describe model 9.1.0 and the recorded 2026 data, with the source-overlap caveat in the table. The scorer reported one missing Mailwoman result, zero missing Pelias results, and 20 missing Photon results among those 297 rows per arm.

## Read the metrics at their measured scope

**Resolve rate** counts queries that returned a coordinate. The Belgian `base` arm's 27 of 30 is a resolve count. Its 24 of 30 bounding-box count answers a narrower country-routing question. Neither count establishes that the house number exists.

**Exact parse** compares all required labeled spans with annotations for an address row or group under a stated scorer. The held-out development replay prints its country and group denominators, but its provenance classes did not admit a release verdict. The current version 7 specification needs identified datasets and a release-matched comparator before its exact-parse requirements can grade model 10.8.0.

**Coordinate accuracy** needs a reference coordinate independent enough for the question being asked. The France panel grades against BAN addresses that supplied its rooftop database, so its distance measures retrieval of BAN rows. The open-truth panel uses open-register rooftop coordinates, but Mailwoman and Pelias also use some of those registers. The result file and source notes must accompany any quoted threshold count.

**The sampling method** determines the population a result can describe. France keeps one sampled address per postcode. Belgium uses curated civic lines. The open-truth report publishes 297 provenance-qualified rows from a 420-row internal panel. Each procedure changes the address mix. None of those panels estimates the error rate of every address submitted to release 11.0.0.

**Throughput** belongs to a model, host, cache state, input set, and concurrency policy. The old reading guide quotes 2,615 ms for 200 France-panel geocodes, while its committed [result file](/benchmarks/fr-ban-results.json) records `elapsedMs: 4485` for that run. The timing scopes are not reconciled here. The 6.3.0 [performance note](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/v11.0.0/docs/engineering/reference/performance.mdx) states no parsed-address denominator and includes no committed replay fixture for that sweep. No 11.0.0 throughput figure is published by this index.
