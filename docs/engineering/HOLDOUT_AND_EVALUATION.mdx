# Held-out data and evaluation specification

**Adopted 2026-10-24** ([#2823](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/issues/2823)). This page revises
the proposal of 2026-10-22 under its review's nine acceptance criteria and writes in the four decisions the
operator made on 2026-10-23. Section 11 lists which criterion changed which section. The evidence behind each
rule comes from the training postmortem of 2026-10-20 to 2026-10-22. Each measurement is quoted with its
denominator where it decides a rule.

## 1. Purpose

The corpus serves two jobs that compete for the same rows. Training learns address structure from rows: which
tokens are a street, a locality, a region, a postcode, and the order each country writes them in. Evaluation
decides whether a new model may replace the published one by scoring both on rows that show how they behave on
addresses users will send. A row held out for evaluation is a row the model never learns from, and a row trained
on no longer measures generalization. This page sets how rows are divided, what checks hold the division correct,
what a candidate is compared against, and when a training run may start.

## 2. Principles

**P1. Training gets the data by default, and evaluation keeps the evidence its claims need.** A row is held out
only when a registered evaluation claim needs it. Evaluation reserves the smallest sample that meets the coverage
and precision each registered requirement states, and the remainder trains. Independent evaluation data already
frozen stays in place until its replacement is frozen and audited. When the available rows cannot satisfy both
jobs, the conflict is recorded in the allocation report (section 4.5) and the claim is reported as unmeasured,
rather than either requirement being weakened in silence.

Motivation: the operator's standing direction is to use the data rather than withhold it over memorization. A
perturbation test on 2026-10-22 moved the published model's exact-parse rate by at most 0.021 when held-out rows
were altered off its training text. This page reads that as a descriptive paired effect over the rows tested. A
claim about memorization in general needs a controlled training comparison, and that comparison remains to be
run.

**P2. Hold out places below the region, and keep every capability's training support.** Held-out groups are
geographic and sit below the first-level region wherever the data permits. Training keeps measured support for
every required component role, every parent region, every script and every layout. Evaluation may introduce
unseen lexical values where lexical generalization is the capability under test. A population for which these
cannot hold together is reported in the allocation report, and the report is where the decision is made.

Motivation: corpus v0.8.0 held out whole first-level regions, among them VT, ND and WY in the US, Asturias and
the Balearic Islands in ES, Trento and Bolzano in IT, and Kerala, Cornwall, TAS and NT. Those regions then had zero region spans in training, and the model relabeled them as localities.
Measured on the US golden set, 2,324 of 2,660 rows with a region lie on VT, ND or WY. Correct rows on that subset
fell from 1,902 to 1,821, and the remaining 336 rows scored 254 correct under both models. The `us.region` score
fell from 89.5 to 87.8 ([#2777](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/issues/2777)). Those two are
measured scores. The registered floor at the time was 89.4.

**P3. Evaluation readiness is structural first, then numerical.** Before a release-directed run, the evaluator
runs on the published model with the dataset the run will be graded on and answers three questions apart. First,
can every required check read enough admissible evidence under the registered rules. Second, does the published
model meet the absolute requirements. Third, what candidate identities are still pending. The first must be yes.
The second informs the work and doesn't block a candidate meant to repair a published deficiency. The third is
recorded as pending and required before any verdict.

Motivation: four from-scratch runs, about 17 GPU-hours, were trained before anyone checked that release
specification v7 could return `pass` on this data. It couldn't. The candidate artifact was unregistered, and the
comparison set for US, ES and IT was empty because the published model had trained on every row those countries
hold in this corpus family.

**P4. Measure once, cheaply, before acting, and publish the allocation.** A change to the split, the corpus or
the recipe is measured on the CPU before it ships, and the measurement is an allocation report (section 4.5) a
reader can inspect. A from-scratch training run is never the first measurement of a corpus change.

**P5. One variable per run, a single control first, and three runs per arm before a change is judged.** On a new
corpus, one unchanged-recipe control is trained and graded before any sweep. That control is a first
measurement. Replication comes from further runs under section 7.2, where a corpus or recipe change is judged on
three runs of the candidate against three runs of the control.

Motivation for the seed rule: v8.1.0 trained with seed 42 against seed 43 flipped 87 regression-board rows of
1,277 and 28 blocking cases of 643, a 25-pass gap. The corpus change under test, v8.2.0 against v8.1.0 at seed 42,
flipped 93 rows with a 35-pass gap. One seed per run cannot tell those apart. The seed-noise floor rests on one
seed pair, so its own variance is unknown. Three runs per arm (section 7.2) bound it for the change at hand
without claiming more.

## 3. Identities

The split and its audits use five identities, recorded apart because each answers a different question:

1. **Row identity**, `source_id`, the key the split attribute files are applied by.
2. **Record identity**, the part of `source_id` before its last `#`. It keeps a record's rendered variants
   together in validation and test.
3. **Geographic identity**, the place key a holdout selects: a postcode district, else a locality spelling folded
   with `foldNFKCWhitespace`, with the region as context. A folded spelling merges homonymous localities and
   separates aliases of one place. That limitation stands until places are resolved with geographic context, and
   the allocation report lists the merged keys it finds.
4. **Statistical group**, the unit uncertainty is estimated over, the held-out place or the frozen dataset's
   group, never the row.
5. **Overlap relation**, the audit of exposure to training data, by record identity and by folded text.

The held-out decision reads a row's components before the record-based validation and test assignment, so a
variant that changes the chosen key can change that earlier decision. The record-ancestry audit (section 4.4)
checks every record's rows across train, validation and test.

## 4. The row split and its checks

Every row receives exactly one of `train`, `val` or `test`. The assignment is deterministic and depends only on
the row's content and the split policy in `packages/corpus/lib/utils/split.ts`. The policy identity is a hash
over the implementation, the resolved holdout tables, the normalization rules and every population-dependent
choice, and every split record states the hash it was made under.

### 4.1 The declared holdout path

A country may declare small places to hold out, as postcode districts or localities. A key is a place below the
first-level region. A region name, or a postcode prefix that covers a whole region, isn't a valid key. EG
`postcode:42` once held out 1,000 of Port Said's 1,008 rows and in effect the governorate. Keys are chosen by a
deterministic walk over the corpus, never by hand. The walk targets the country's current held-out share and lets
the share fall rather than adding places. No first-level region with at least `HOLDOUT_CAP_MIN_ROWS` rows may lose
more than `heldOutRegionCap(share)`, `min(0.25, max(0.05, 3 × share))`, of its rows or of its no-postcode rows. A
spelling that matches a region's surface form is excluded from the candidates.

### 4.2 The hashed holdout path and its region cap

A country without a declared list holds out one place in fifty, chosen by a hash of its key (venue, else
locality, else postcode). The region cap applies to each hash-selected place on its own. Its rows inside one
region are divided by that region's rows, and its rows with no region by the country's rows with no region. A
denominator under `HOLDOUT_CAP_MIN_ROWS` isn't checked. A place over `heldOutRegionCap(0.02)`, which is 0.06,
trains instead and isn't replaced. Measured on v0.8.1 this returns 9 places in 7 countries, 2,067 rows, both EG
governorate postcodes among them.

### 4.3 The population-sized source cap

A hash-selected place may hold only part of one source's held-out rows in its country. That part is a ceiling
that scales with how much of the country the place is, so a small or less important place is represented in
proportion rather than at a fixed share. The operator decided this on 2026-10-23, and a flat quarter remains
as the fallback described below.

The rule's form: for a source `S` in country `C`, a hash-selected place `p` may hold at most
`max(floor, k × share(p, C))` of `S`'s held-out rows in `C`, where `share(p, C)` is the place's population
divided by the country's. The place's population is the Who's On First `population` of the admin place the key
resolves to, read from the admin gazetteer. A postcode key takes the population of the locality with the most
rows under that postcode. The country's population is `COUNTRY_POPULATION` in
`packages/codex/lib/country/population.ts`, derived from GeoNames. A key with no population reading, or a
country GeoNames declines to estimate, falls back to the flat quarter and is listed as unsized in the allocation
report. A place over its ceiling trains instead and isn't replaced.

The two parameters, `floor` and `k`, are registered only after the allocation report in section 4.5 has been
produced over every country for a candidate pair, with the newly returned places inspected. A threshold fitted
to the places that failed under the flat quarter would measure that quarter's defect rather than the
distribution. The motivating case stays: v0.8.1's draw held out `locality:new delhi`, 82% of India's held-out
OSM rows and the only OSM training rows ending `, New Delhi`, and the model then read the city as a
`neighbourhood` (confidence 0.94 against 0.40 to 0.80). New Delhi holds about 1.2% of India's population, so
under any `k` below sixty-five the rule returns it to training.

Known cost, accepted under P1: small countries lose most or all of their held-out rows. After the region cap and
the flat quarter on v0.8.1, AX and TZ have 0, HT and SV 1, the Dominican Republic 3, HK and SM 6, and EG, MK and
KG 8. None is a required country, and each is marked experimental for evaluation purposes.

### 4.4 Checks that run before a manifest exists

Each check below belongs to the step that admits the corpus manifest: it runs once per corpus, on the real rows,
and refuses to admit the manifest when it fails. Only the held-out span check runs there today, and section 10 gives
the state of the others. The parquet writer stages its manifest as `MANIFEST.unchecked.json`.
`admitParquetManifest` in `packages/corpus/lib/build/holdout-check.ts` runs the held-out span check over it,
writes `HOLDOUT_CHECK.json` beside it with every finding, and moves it to `MANIFEST.json` only on a pass. A
corpus the check refuses therefore has no manifest a training run can read, and the top-level manifest records
when the check ran, the countries it read and the files it counted.

1. **Held-out span check** (`packages/corpus/lib/tools/overlay/held-out-spans.ts`). Two-sided: no train row
   holds a declared locality or postcode prefix, and every first-level region of every declared country keeps a
   train region-span share of at least half its country's median, over all rows and over no-postcode rows. A
   region-name key is refused. It took 1,344 s on v0.8.1 and runs inside the build.
2. **Concentration check.** For every source with 1,000 or more rows in a country, every held-out place stays
   under its ceiling from section 4.3, and the source keeps at least 100 held-out rows unless P1 gave them to
   training.
3. **Record-ancestry audit.** Every record's rows sit on one side of train against held out, and its variants
   never straddle validation and test. Development and confirmation sets are disjoint by record group.
4. **Policy identity.** The manifest binds the source file hashes, the row identities, the policy hash, the
   attribute hashes and the audit results. The loader rejects a missing attribute, a duplicate assignment, a
   mismatched source identity and an unsupported version.
5. **Label-support floors and exposure receipts** are re-derived from each new corpus, never copied forward. A
   label's validation floor is half its measured support rounded down, with no floor under 20 rows. An exposure
   receipt is re-derived from the corpus's own epoch-mixture audit. A receipt copied from an older corpus fails
   on supply the new split moved, as `fr-annuaire-education-overseas` did at 1,278 of a stale 1,300.

### 4.5 The allocation report

Before any training run on a new split, a report compares the proposed split with the preceding policy. For every
affected country, source, region and required capability it shows the available rows, the retained training
rows, the held-out records and groups, the change from the preceding policy, and the same figures over
no-postcode rows. It lists every place a cap returned to training with its denominators, the capabilities
recovered in training, the evaluation coverage lost, the keys found to merge homonymous places, and the unsized
keys of section 4.3. A corpus-consistency assertion (a floor re-derived from the new corpus passes) is reported
apart from a product-quality requirement (a required capability's supply fell against the previous corpus), so a
reduced supply stays visible when a derived floor passes.

## 5. Building a new split

A split-policy change doesn't rebuild the corpus. The store holds every source's parsed rows as parquet, and a
re-split writes a split-attribute file beside each parquet file and a new manifest. Measured: 853 files, 675.5
million rows, 76 minutes on 8 workers, peak 937 MiB per worker, 391 MB of new files, against 4 h 04 min for the
last full rebuild. The loader applies the attribute files as row filters at no measured throughput cost in that
configuration, 21.2 batches per second on v0.8.1 against 20.6 on v0.8.0. That benchmark covers one loader and
storage setup and doesn't establish equivalence under others.

### 5.1 Ordered shapes, closure supply, and the retention floor

The split and every allocation report count addresses by ordered shape, under four definitions versioned here
(#2893).

1. **Independent group.** One real address record: `coalesce(base_source_id, source_id)`. Every rendered
   variant of a record shares the group, and the group shares one split assignment. A real-world address present
   in two sources is two groups until a cross-source dedup exists, and the allocation report lists them.
2. **Ordered shape.** A row's rendered shape is its exact `span_tags` sequence, consecutive duplicates kept.
3. **Shape closure.** A group's closure is every order-preserving subset of every rendered sequence the group
   holds. A sequence of k components reaches 2^k − 1 shapes: `28 Hershberger Ln, Grantsville, MD 21536-1004`
   reaches its full shape, the same shape without the postcode, and `locality → region`.
4. **Eligible population and closure supply.** Per country, all groups in the corpus build, collated across
   sources. A shape's closure supply is the count of groups whose closure contains it. Supply is counted on the
   closure rather than on rendered rows, because recipes render few permutations: on v0.8.8-nppes-overlay,
   0.19% of 112,125,570 groups render more than one sequence.

**Retention.** A holdout allocates a country's held-out groups across shapes in proportion to closure supply,
subject to three rules in this precedence: the retention floor, geographic integrity, proportionality.

1. **Retention floor.** A country×shape whose post-holdout training remainder would fall below the floor keeps
   every group in training, and its held-out accuracy is reported as unmeasured until independent evaluation
   data exists. The floor is registered at **10 independent groups** (2026-11-01, #2893). The allocation report
   over v0.8.8-nppes-overlay (`/mnt/mw/audits/2893/supply/allocation-report-floor-10.md`, with
   `SPLIT_RETENTION.floor-10.json`) measured the registration: 3,975 of 10,345 combinations sit below the floor,
   retention keeps 997 rendered rows in training that the split held out, and all 24 combinations with zero
   training groups return to training. A floor of 100 would have left 1,223 combinations that currently hold
   evaluation rows without any. A floor of 10 limits that loss to 274 combinations. The registration followed the census rather than
   the failures: the candidate table was priced over every country before the value was chosen.
2. **Launch check.** A country×shape whose total closure supply is under the floor fails the pre-launch audit of
   section 8.
3. **Proportionality and shortfall.** Where geography prevents the proportional allocation, the allocation
   report states the target share, the actual share and the cause per combo, as section 4.5 already requires of
   returned places. The census of 2026-11-01 measured the failure the rule prevents: 24 combos held 115 val and
   test rows with zero training rows, the largest French Polynesia's 69 `street → dependent_locality → postcode
→ locality` rows, all in Pirae under held-out postcode 98716.

The same definitions cover the Who's On First language permutations: a language rendering is a member of the
group's closure, so a per-country language shape enters the census, the allocation and the floor like any other
shape. The census also counts groups whose variants straddle two splits — 1,025,841 on v0.8.8-nppes-overlay,
against the record-ancestry audit of section 4.4 — so the re-split that applies this section repairs them.

## 6. Evaluation

### 6.1 Provenance classes

Every held-out row is audited against the training corpora of both the published model and the candidate.
`verified_unseen` sits in neither. `unseen_by_candidate` sits in the published model's training data only.
`known_overlap` and `unknown` are admitted to no check. A minimum check admits `verified_unseen` and
`unseen_by_candidate` rows. A comparative check admits `verified_unseen` only.

### 6.2 The comparator

A candidate is graded against the published package the users install: the weights npm serves under the
`@mailwoman/neural-weights-*` version the release config states at grading time, identified by the sha256 of
its `model.onnx`. The published model is the comparison for every gain and loss reported, and its own past
answers do not define a minimum.

### 6.3 Evaluation rows from unsampled source rows

A corpus build samples a source's rows. The rows it leaves unsampled on disk are rows no training run of that
corpus family has read. Evaluation may draw from them, under two guards read from the support index that
`packages/mailwoman/tools/dev-tools/corpus/support-index.run.ts` writes beside a corpus manifest and
`mwdev_support` reads: the train rows per country for each tag set, and each value's rows under its own tag and
under every other tag.

- **Variety.** The draw is stratified by tag set within each country, so the evaluation set holds every address
  shape the country's training rows hold, in proportion to the on-disk supply rather than to the sampled corpus.
- **Supply.** A tag set whose on-disk supply for a country is under twice the draw's share for that shape keeps
  every row for training, and the draw takes none of it. A row drawn for evaluation is excluded from every later
  training draw of its source, so it stays unseen. The guard keeps that exclusion from taking a country's last
  rows of a shape.

A drawn set is frozen, audited by the overlap relation against both training lineages, inspected for labels, and
checked for representativeness against the required address forms, because newly sampled rows can
disproportionately represent newly developed areas or improved source coverage.

### 6.4 The comparison problem for US, ES and IT

The published model 10.7.0 trained on v0.7.10, and that corpus's holdout leaked: 716,356 VT, ND and WY rows
reached training, 95.3% of them as lowercased `+case-lower` variants that a case-sensitive match missed. The
audited comparison sets for US, ES and IT lack sufficient admissible evidence against 10.7.0. Whether every row
of those countries in this corpus family was seen is established only by a full-family scan, and the
perturbation report's positive control left one row of 2,111 unmatched. The fix is an evaluation source outside
both corpora, a newer snapshot of a source the adapters already read, kept only where absent from both lineages
by id and by folded text. The operator's constraint of 2026-10-22 is unchanged: the pull is deferred, and when
needed it is US only. ES and IT therefore remain unresolved requirements under the nine-country objective, and a
US-only acquisition is reported as leaving them unresolved.

### 6.5 Confirmation access

The development set guides work. The release decision is read once, on the confirmation set. Preflight may
inspect confirmation metadata, coverage and provenance. Baseline scoring of the published model on confirmation
is allowed once per specification version and recorded. Only the release report reads row-level outcomes on
confirmation. Every confirmation read is recorded in the next specification's rationale. A candidate that read
confirmation and failed retires that set for recipe selection, and its successor is drawn and frozen before the
next release-directed run.

## 7. Candidate grading and the three-run rule

### 7.1 The grading

1. **Self-control.** A staged copy of the published weights graded against the installed weights differs on 0
   board rows.
2. **Release requirements**, the current specification: minimum checks per required country and capability, and
   the comparative objective against the comparator of section 6.2.
3. **Promotion battery floors** not yet retired. A floor the published model itself misses is a conflict to
   investigate. A scorer correction, an answer-key revision and an accepted quality reduction are three different
   decisions, each with its own recorded reason.
4. **The regression board** as findings. Every regressed row is explained with its address. The board's net count
   is a finding and never a verdict, because it sits inside seed noise.
5. **Provenance split** of every held-out rate.

### 7.2 Three runs per arm before a corpus or recipe change is judged

A corpus or recipe change is judged on three runs of the candidate, graded beside three runs of the control on the
previous corpus or recipe, with every run on the same trainer. A run is a seed, or a repeat of one seed: on the
A100, two runs of one config and one seed disagreed on 5.1% of battery rows, and two seeds on 6.5% to 7.6%. The comparison is
read with `battery/groups.run.ts` over a battery report that holds all six runs.

- A suite or the battery total counts as changed when the tool's two-sided p is below 0.05. Its standard error adds
  the between-run variance of the two group means to the row-sampling variance of their difference, with
  `k_a + k_b - 2` degrees of freedom.
- A country counts as changed when its Holm-adjusted p, over every country with at least 200 decision rows, is
  below 0.05.
- A difference that doesn't meet the bar is reported as unestablished, in place of a gain or a loss. A difference
  smaller than the cell's smallest detectable difference for the runs used is unestablished by construction.
- The tool lists the rows that move under the all-but-one agreement rule, and the difference of every regrouping
  of the pooled runs. A shape that moves in the observed split and also in splits that mix the two arms is noise.
- A board flip count is a finding to read, never the verdict.

The rule comes from #2869's Phase 2 and Phase 3, measured 2026-10-31 on the Rust trainer at 90,000 steps
(int8, 26,743 battery decision rows). Three control seeds on `v0.8.7-overlay` scored 73.6%, 72.7% and 72.3%. Three
runs on `v0.8.8-nppes-overlay`, seeds 42, 42 again and 43, scored 70.6%, 71.8% and 72.4%. The two seed 42 runs
disagree on 1,368 rows (5.1%), and pairs of control seeds on 1,750 to 2,031 (6.5% to 7.6%). The pooled standard
deviation of the run rates is 0.81 points on the total and 1.35 on `osm-venue-with-address`. With it, the smallest
difference a comparison detects (two-sided, alpha 0.05, power 0.8) is:

| Cell                        | 2 runs per arm | 3 runs | 4 runs | 5 runs |
| --------------------------- | -------------: | -----: | -----: | -----: |
| all suites                  |            4.4 |    2.5 |    2.0 |    1.7 |
| `osm-venue-with-address`    |            7.3 |    4.2 |    3.3 |    2.8 |
| `osm-district-before-city`  |            1.9 |    1.1 |    0.9 |    0.8 |
| `osm-region-after-locality` |           30.2 |   17.1 |   13.4 |   11.4 |

Two runs per arm therefore cannot resolve the 1.3-point difference the Phase 2 corpus change produced (p = 0.13 with
three runs per arm). `osm-region-after-locality` swings by more
than 10 points between control seeds (95.2%, 83.7% and 92.2%), so a single comparison cannot read that suite.

A run on the Rust trainer costs about $4.30 on an A100-40GB (#2887). A comparison against a control whose three
runs already exist costs three candidate runs, about $13. A comparison that needs new control runs too costs six,
about $26.

### 7.3 When a screen run decides

A screen run reads an arm at an intermediate checkpoint of one seed in place of the arm's final result.
A screen decides a comparison between two arms only when all three of these hold:

1. **The two arms share their stage structure up to the screen step.** Neither arm changes its data or its
   schedule after the screen step. A primer stage followed by a second stage fails this, because the second stage
   changes what the first stage taught.
2. **The screen gap exceeds the registered margin** `m = max(0.05, 2s)`, where `s` is the larger seed difference of
   the two arms on the measure, on every seed screened.
3. **A second seed's screen agrees in sign.**

Otherwise the comparison runs both arms in full under section 7.2.

The rule comes from the 30,000-step checkpoints of the three #2857 arms, exported locally from the Modal volume's
checkpoints and graded in fp32 on the held-out structure board (measured 2026-10-29). H is the
`wof-heldout-order` rate on 556 rows:

| Arm                        | Screen checkpoint                | H at the screen, s42 / s43 | H at the final step, s42 / s43 |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| B, one stage, 90,000 steps | step 30,000                      | 0.642 / 0.626              | 0.656 / 0.653                  |
| A, primer then base        | base step 30,000 (60,000 in all) | 0.610 / 0.613              | 0.601 / 0.613                  |
| A, primer then base        | primer step 30,000               | 0.683 / 0.673              | 0.601 / 0.613                  |
| C, one stage, 60,000 steps | step 30,000                      | 0.426 / 0.396              | 0.439 / 0.424                  |

The final two-seed order is B, then A, then C. Against it:

- The screen of B against C clears the margin on both seeds (gaps 0.216 and 0.230) and agrees with the final order.
- The screen of B against A, each at its own step 30,000, gives the final order's sign on both seeds. Its gaps
  of 0.032 and 0.013 are inside the margin, so under rule 2 the comparison needs the full arms. The final gap is 0.055 and 0.040.
- The screen of A at its primer's step 30,000, the same number of total steps as B's screen, ranks A first on both
  seeds by 0.041 and 0.047. The final order ranks A second. Rule 1 excludes that screen.

The comparison battery (`battery/battery.run.ts`, 26,743 decision rows, percent right) reads the same checkpoints
differently:

| Arm                   | At the screen, s42 / s43 | At the final step, s42 / s43 |
| --------------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| B, step 30,000        | 66.7 / 68.7              | 69.7 / 69.2                  |
| A, base step 30,000   | 69.9 / 70.9              | 70.1 / 69.6                  |
| A, primer step 30,000 | 0.0 / 0.0                | 70.1 / 69.6                  |
| C, step 30,000        | 67.4 / 67.4              | 67.9 / 67.9                  |

The battery registers no margin, so `m` is twice the largest final seed difference of the two arms, 1.0 point.

- A against B: the screen gaps (3.2 and 2.2 points) clear `m` and agree in sign. The final gaps (0.4 and 0.4) agree
  in sign but sit inside the seed spread. The screen decides the order correctly and overstates its size.
- B against C: the screen gaps are −0.7 and +1.3, opposite in sign, so under rule 3 the comparison runs both arms
  in full. The final order puts B ahead by 1.8 and 1.3.
- The WOF-only primer parses no battery row right. Rule 1 excludes its screen.

Arms A, B and C cast their parameters to bf16. A run that keeps fp32 master weights (#2871) follows a different
optimization path, so whether its step-30,000 checkpoint ranks arms the way its final step does is unknown. The
rule is unverified for that lineage. The first comparison inside it, the Phase 2 pair of #2869 against the
fp32-master control, can check it: grade both arms' step-30,000 checkpoints beside their final steps before the
screen decides any fp32-master comparison.

The two measures disagree on the final order of A and B: the structure board puts B ahead by 0.040 to 0.055, and
the battery puts A ahead by 0.4 points. A screen inherits whichever measure it is read on, so a screen decision
identifies its measure. These are three arm pairs, each with two seeds, so the rule rests on 12 screen readings and
stays provisional until another arm family is measured.

## 8. When a training run launches

A run launches only when all of the following hold, checked in this order:

1. The corpus manifest was admitted with the checks of section 4.4 passing, and the allocation report of section
   4.5 exists for the split.
2. The evaluator, run on the published model under the current specification with the run's evaluation dataset,
   answers yes to the first question of P3, and every pending candidate identity is recorded as pending.
3. The configuration's floors and receipts were re-derived from this corpus.
4. The corpus-receipt preflight passes on Modal's CPU before a GPU is allocated.
5. The run has a written prediction: which checks it should move, by how much, and on which rows.
6. When the corpus is new, the run is the single control (P5). A corpus change's candidate runs under section 7.2 follow the control.

A bounded diagnostic run, one that will never be graded for release, may skip items 2 and 5 and is recorded as
diagnostic in its configuration. Runs launch with automatic resume when the checkpoint matches the same run,
corpus, tokenizer and configuration and restores optimizer and scheduler state. A changed configuration or a
completed learning-rate schedule requires an explicit new-run decision.
