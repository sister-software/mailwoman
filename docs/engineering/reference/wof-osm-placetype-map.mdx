# WOF placetypes ↔ OSM tags

Who's On First's placetype vocabulary and OpenStreetMap's tagging scheme both describe places, but
they are not two dialects of one language. WOF has a **single-axis hierarchy of place kinds**; OSM
has **free-form key/value tags** with an administrative hierarchy expressed as a country-relative
number. Mapping between them is useful — it is how `terminal` and `gate` reached our venue-structure
vocabulary — but three structural mismatches make a naive table dangerous.

Written 2026-08-02 while extending the span proposer's designator vocabulary. Confidence is flagged
per row: OSM conventions are firm in some places and genuinely contested in others.

## The three mismatches to know before reading the table

**1. `place=locality` is a FALSE FRIEND, and it is the most dangerous row here.** In WOF a
`locality` is a settlement — a city, town or village, the backbone of the address hierarchy. In OSM,
`place=locality` means **a named place with no population**: a spot on a map that people call
something, explicitly not a settlement. The two mean nearly opposite things. A WOF locality maps to
OSM `place=city|town|village|hamlet`. Anyone mapping these mechanically will silently import
uninhabited places as cities.

**2. `admin_level` is country-relative, so the administrative rows cannot map to fixed numbers.**
OSM's `admin_level=2` is reliably the country, but 4/6/7/8 mean different things in different
countries by explicit design — a London borough is `admin_level=8`, while a New York borough sits
higher in a differently-shaped hierarchy. Every administrative row below is therefore "typically",
never "equals".

**3. WOF's venue-INTERIOR placetypes have no counterpart in OSM's place hierarchy at all.** `arcade`,
`concourse`, `wing`, `enclosure` are not places to OSM — they are building interior, modelled by the
separate **Simple Indoor Tagging** scheme (`indoor=room|corridor|area|level`, `building:part=*`,
`level=*`). This is the mismatch that matters most for us: it means the vocabulary we want for
sub-venue parsing lives in OSM's _indoor_ scheme, not its _place_ scheme.

## What this changed for us

The venue-structure designator vocabulary (`core/resources/whosonfirst/placetypes/venue-structure.ts`)
takes `arcade`, `building`, `campus`, `concourse`, `enclosure`, `installation`, `wing` from WOF and
adds `terminal` and `gate` from OSM's `aeroway` key — precisely because WOF has no placetype for
either and they are the two commonest sub-venue designators in real airport addresses.

The table suggests where that vocabulary could grow, and where it should not:

- **Plausible additions from OSM Simple Indoor Tagging**: `level` (overlaps Pub 28's `FLOOR`) and
  `hall`. Both are written in real venue addresses.
- **Not worth adding**: `corridor`, `room` (already Pub 28), `area` — `corridor` and `area` are
  mapping primitives nobody writes on an envelope.
- **Nothing from the administrative rows.** Those are the pair-index and census program's territory,
  and `admin_level` being country-relative is exactly why we key that work on WOF's hierarchy rather
  than OSM's numbers.

## Why we lean on WOF for hierarchy and OSM for interiors

The split is not arbitrary. WOF's single-axis hierarchy with explicit parent links is what makes
`(child, parent)` pair extraction possible at all — the campaign's entire mechanism assumes a place
knows its ancestors. OSM has no equivalent: containment is inferred from geometry, and the
administrative levels it does carry are country-relative. Conversely OSM's indoor scheme describes
building interiors in a detail WOF's seven interior placetypes never attempt.

Each source is used where its model is strongest, which is also why neither is treated as
authoritative for the other's territory.
