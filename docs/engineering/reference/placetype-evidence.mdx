# Placetype evidence — the projection table and the census design

How the WOF placetype vocabulary meets the `ComponentTag` grammar, why the decoder takes _nudges_
from the hierarchy rather than constraints, and the design the pair-index arc is the first
production instance of. Codified 2026-08-01 from the hierarchy-evidence conversations (the
"Queens under New York" formulation) and the GB dependent-locality arc's receipts.

## The two ontologies

WOF placetypes (~33: `borough`, `microhood`, `dependency`, `concourse`, …) describe what a place
**is**. `ComponentTag` (see [SCHEMA](./SCHEMA.mdx)) describes what role a span plays in an
address's **grammar**. They are different vocabularies with a many-to-one **projection**, and the
projection — not the placetype — is what the decoder can use. SCHEMA.mdx already fixes the
canonical example: `borough` is not a tag; `dependent_locality` is the umbrella term borough
projects onto.

**The rule: no placetype gets its own tag; every placetype gets a projection, and hierarchy
evidence is computed over projections.**

## From pairs to the census

The shipped **placetype-pair index** (`pair-index-gb.bin`, `pair-index-nz.bin`) is the sharpest
instance of the general idea: known (child surface, parent surface) memberships, applied as a soft
logit bias at decode time. Its three-way ablation is the composition proof (0/69 and 3/69 for
model-alone and index-alone; 69/69 together).

The general form is the **placetype census**: per gazetteer node, the distribution of its
children's _projected_ tags ("this parent: 5 borough-children, 90 neighbourhoods, 0 dependent
localities"). That turns a globally-rare tag into a conditionally-common one exactly where it
should be — `dependent_locality`'s global prior is tiny, its prior _given this subtree_ is large.

**Built 2026-08-01** (hierarchy campaign R4c) as **PCN1** — `neural/placetype-census.ts` carries
writer and reader in one file, the same discipline as PIX1 and PCB1, and `mailwoman gazetteer
census` builds it from the WOF admin DB through the projection table above (executable as
`PLACETYPE_PROJECTION` in `mailwoman/gazetteer-pipeline/placetype-census.ts`, so a placetype the
table has not been extended for makes the build throw rather than silently go uncounted). The GB
artifact is 5,577 nodes / 137 KB over 33,899 counted child links.

What the offline probe measured, and what it did not:

- A census hit is **real conditional evidence**. Of GB's 16,987 distinct locality-class surfaces,
  33.2% carry any dependent-locality child — so a hit rules out two thirds of the parent
  population — and against PPD post towns (25.7M rows, a source the census never read) **74.3% of
  rows** land in a covered town, 77.5% among rows that actually carry a dependent locality.
- The complementary measurement is about the pair index: it resolves **11.6%** of WOF's 22,843 GB
  dependent-locality links. The long tail is most of the space, and it is what the census is for.
- **The census is parent-scoped and cannot do span-level work.** On the 56-row law-1 confound
  board — directional neighbourhood surfaces that open venue names — every row shares the London
  parent with the true positives, so the census has dependent-locality mass on 100% of them. A
  census bias alone would lift the venue span exactly as much as the real dependent locality. This
  is the artifact's ceiling, not a tuning problem.

It therefore ships **without a `delta`**: the header field exists, is optional, and stays unset
until a calibration rung composes the census with span-level evidence and measures a magnitude.
Within-node share turned out to carry no information at all (WOF rarely parents a locality under a
locality, so covered nodes read ~100% dependent-locality across the board); presence and magnitude
across the parent population are the statistics that do.

**The observability rung (2026-08-05).** The census is now LOADED and PROBED at runtime, and still
biases nothing. `resolvePlacetypeCensusPath` (`neural/weights.ts`) finds
`placetype-census-<cc>.bin` at the build-local data root — deliberately not in the weights tarballs,
which would cost 137–165 KB per locale for an artifact nothing reads back — and the placetype-pair
prior probes it for the PARENT half of every (child, parent) candidate it tries, on the same fold.
What comes out is a `placetypeCensus` record on the parse trace (`{parent, childTagsPresent, lift}`
per known parent, plus the probed-parent count as the denominator), whose `applied` is hard-coded
`false`. The decode is byte-identical with the census present or absent, asserted at both the prior
and the full-decode level in `neural/placetype-census-observability.test.ts`. That test is the
contract: a calibration δ has to be measured off these traces, not slipped in.

Two things the first real traces showed. `shoreditch, london` (en-GB) reads `london` at lift 1.52×
on `dependent_locality` — the evidence is there and it is the right sign. `astoria, queens, ny`
(en-US) records **zero** observations over three probed parents: `queens` has no census node,
because `PARENT_PLACETYPES` is `["locality", "localadmin"]` and Queens is a `borough`. The US pair
index's characteristic edge — a dependent locality under a BOROUGH, the case PIX2 was built to
express — is structurally outside the census's parent vocabulary today. A calibration rung that
reads only GB traces will not see that. (Re-measured on the post-rebuild gazetteer, 2026-08-05: GB
5,577 nodes / 136,675 bytes / 33,936 links; US 7,532 nodes / 164,764 bytes / 123,909 links.)

Why the census does not simply become more pair entries: a pair entry _asserts_ a surface is a
dependent locality, so each batch needs a venue-confound board before it ships. A census node
asserts nothing about any surface — it can only tilt a reading the model already entertains under a
parent it already identified. That is what makes it the safe carrier for a tail no batch-by-batch
boarding process would ever finish.

## The doctrine constraints (standing, non-negotiable)

1. **Positive evidence only.** Presence boosts; absence stays neutral. A subtree with no boroughs
   must never _forbid_ borough — gazetteer absence is usually coverage, not fact (WOF Japan is
   point-geometry; the meaning-of-zero rule).
2. **Bias, never mask.** The model owns segmentation; the hierarchy contributes soft evidence.
   Hard matching is where venue-confound false positives live (window-mode measured 52% FP; gated
   segment mode is why the shipped floor is sub-2%).
3. **Per-locale conventions still gate expectations.** One model serves every locale; what a
   locale _expects_ to emit stays governed by its conventions (codex address-system conventions).
4. **Locale-scoped artifacts must be provably inert elsewhere.** en-us ships no pair index and a
   dedicated test asserts GB-shaped input takes NO pair bias through en-us — a packaging seatbelt,
   not a doctrine. The US joins the census program through its own instances (boroughs first).
   Measured 2026-08-01 in the other direction too: an experimental US index misses all five GB
   canonical pairs (Shoreditch/London, Nine Elms/London, Clapham/London, Camden/London,
   Didsbury/Manchester), so the browser's `us` fallback for postcode-less queries would not
   manufacture GB bias if a US artifact shipped.

## Why en-us has no pair index today (and why that's sequencing, not doctrine)

The GB/NZ indexes exist because those POSTAL formats carry the dependent-locality/suburb line and
national registers (HM Land Registry PPD, LINZ) provide millions of pair rows. The US format has
no such line — USPS routes city/state/ZIP — so there is no postal register to harvest and no mail
-correctness stake. But the schema's umbrella term means US dependent localities exist (NYC's five
boroughs), and WOF supplies the pairs directly (211 boroughs with parents, globally).

**Corrected 2026-08-01 (campaign R5).** This section previously said the US instance was gated on
"the tag's _contextual_ aliveness for US". That was wrong, and it was wrong about GB too. Three
measurements, in the order that settles it:

1. **Nothing suppresses the tag per country.** The decoder has exactly one hard emission mask
   (`-1e9`) and it carries a single row: FR `street_suffix`. `dependent_locality` appears in no
   `forbiddenTags` table, TS or Python. `componentsSupported` omits the tag for en-US, but its only
   consumer validates the policy registry — nothing reads it to mask emissions.
2. **The tag is dead in the model, and dead UNIFORMLY.** Raw pre-prior logits rank
   `B-dependent_locality` 11th–15th of 25 in every locale — including the GB inputs that parse
   correctly today (`Shoreditch` 0.14%, rank 14, against `locality` at 31.3%). US surfaces carry
   MORE raw mass than the GB ones that work. The en-GB model card had already measured the deficit
   as "large but UNIFORM (~7.0 logits mean)"; the origin is a 0.3 class weight on
   `B/I-dependent_locality` carried from v0.5.1 through v0.8.0, since corrected to 1.0 but not
   retrained into the shipped lineage.
3. **So GB does not emit because the tag is alive there — it emits because an artifact clears a
   uniform deficit.** A US pair index built from WOF (49,033 pairs; `borough` admitted as a parent
   placetype, since WOF parents US neighbourhoods to the locality rather than the borough) makes
   the US behave exactly like GB: `123 Main St, Park Slope, Brooklyn, NY 11215` goes from
   `locality=Park Slope` with **"Brooklyn" silently dropped** to `dependent_locality=Park Slope,
locality=Brooklyn`. Gauntlet unchanged, 0/60 venue-confound false positives, 60/60 tag-correct
   on a positive board.

The US instance is therefore an ARTIFACT question, not a training question — and so is every other
locale in the campaign's wave 2–4 list. Whether `pair-index-us.bin` ships inside the flagship
en-us package is a packaging and defaults decision (it changes US parse output for every consumer),
not a question about the model.

## Coverage boundaries measured so far (2026-08-01 probe set)

The GB pair source (PPD) is England+Wales and leaves CITY empty for London, so London
neighbourhoods, estates, Northern Ireland and the Republic all sat outside the shipped index by the
source's own construction. **Great Britain is now covered end to end**, entirely from open data:

| rung     | source                                  | pairs      |
| -------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| baseline | HM Land Registry PPD                    | 19,209     |
| R2       | WOF boroughs                            | 15         |
| R3 / R4b | ONSPD wards + WOF London neighbourhoods | 815        |
| R7       | WOF Northern Ireland                    | 87         |
| R8       | WOF Scotland, Wales, England            | 10,708     |
|          | **GB total, cross-checked**             | **30,834** |

Every increment after the baseline cleared a venue-confound board before shipping, and no board has
ever exceeded 0 false positives.

**Northern Ireland's blocker turned out not to exist.** It was deferred for months on "the pair
parent needs post towns" — a licensed Royal Mail table — which was reasoning from GB's _source
shape_ (PPD's parent column happens to be the post town) rather than from what a pair requires.
WOF parents NI neighbourhoods to Belfast, Newtownabbey, Londonderry and Lisburn, which _are_ those
post towns. Worth re-reading any deferred blocker after a premise moves.

Two register-vs-writer distinctions the GB rungs had to make explicit, both general:

- A slash-separated WOF parent (`Londonderry / Derry`) is an **alias set**, not a name. It folds to
  one key matching neither alternative, so such pairs ship _dead_ — present in the artifact,
  incapable of firing. Emit one pair per alternative.
- A civil-parish suffix (`Pontypridd Community`, `Llanelli Rural`) is administrative furniture. The
  register names the parish that way; an address says `Pontypridd`. Strip the suffix rather than
  dropping the pair — 1,545 Welsh pairs would otherwise have been discarded.

**Every locale on the original wave 2–4 list has now shipped.** The blocker was never data — the
pair index is hard-gated on the resolved locale's country, so an artifact inside another locale's
package can never fire, and each locale needed its own **carrier package**: a ~6-file data-only
overlay declaring `baseWeights`. Once that was understood the rest was mechanical.

| locale |   pairs | source       | note                                                  |
| ------ | ------: | ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| GB     |  30,825 | PPD + WOF    | England, Scotland, Wales, NI, London                  |
| US     |  47,878 | WOF          | no postal register carries the line                   |
| DE     |  85,603 | WOF          | Deutsche Post carries no Ortsteil field               |
| FR     | 199,282 | BAN `nom_ld` | the lieu-dit IS the postal line                       |
| IN     | 176,086 | WOF          | 53% of source nodes are deprecated; a city instrument |
| ES     |   3,559 | WOF          |                                                       |
| IT     |   4,026 | WOF          |                                                       |
| NZ     |       — | LINZ         | shipped earlier                                       |

**Ireland is the only one left, and it is the only one ever blocked on something other than
mechanics**: its sources (Tailte Éireann, logainm) need a licence survey before anything can be
harvested.

Three things the non-GB instances established that the GB arc never had to face:

- **The source follows the postal format, not a rule.** France's lieu-dit comes from BAN because it
  IS the postal line; the US, DE, ES, IT and IN take WOF because no postal register carries their
  sub-city line at all.
- **A leading postcode makes the artifact inert.** France, Germany, Spain and Italy write
  `12210 Montpeyroux`, so a parent segment folds to a key no bare-locality entry matches. Each
  needed a `LEADING_POSTCODE_COUNTRIES` entry, and in every case the artifact built correctly and
  changed nothing until it landed.
- **WOF's preferred name is not always the written one.** It stores `Bangalore` for a city written
  `Bengaluru` and `Rome` for one written `Roma`, and the fold does not strip accents, so `Cordoba`
  never matches `Córdoba`. Parent-alias expansion draws from names whose language is official for
  the country plus English, restricted to Latin script.
