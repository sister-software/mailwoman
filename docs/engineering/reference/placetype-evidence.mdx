# Placetype evidence — the projection table and the census design

This page describes how the WOF placetype vocabulary maps onto the `ComponentTag` grammar, why the
decoder takes _nudges_ from the hierarchy rather than constraints, and the design that the pair
index first put into production. It was written on 2026-08-01 from the hierarchy-evidence
conversations (the "Queens under New York" formulation) and the measurements from the GB
dependent-locality work.

## The two ontologies

WOF placetypes (~33: `borough`, `microhood`, `dependency`, `concourse`, …) describe what a place
**is**. `ComponentTag` (see [SCHEMA](./SCHEMA.mdx)) describes the role a span plays in an
address's **grammar**. A **projection** maps several placetypes onto each tag, and the
decoder can use the projection but not the placetype itself. SCHEMA.mdx already gives the
canonical example: `borough` is not a tag, and `dependent_locality` is the umbrella term that
borough projects onto.

**The rule: no placetype gets its own tag. Every placetype gets a projection, and hierarchy
evidence is computed over projections.**

## From pairs to the census

The shipped **placetype-pair index** (`pair-index-gb.bin`, `pair-index-nz.bin`) is the most
specific form of the general idea. It stores known (child surface, parent surface) memberships and
applies them as a soft logit bias at decode time. Its three-way ablation shows that the model and
the index work in combination: 0/69 for the model alone, 3/69 for the index alone, and 69/69
together.

The general form is the **placetype census**. For each gazetteer node, it records the distribution
of its children's _projected_ tags ("this parent: 5 borough-children, 90 `neighbourhood` rows, 0
dependent localities"). The census turns a tag that is rare globally into one that is common in the
right context. `dependent_locality`'s global prior is tiny, but its prior _given this subtree_ is
large.

**Built 2026-08-01** (hierarchy campaign R4c) as **PCN1**. `neural/placetype-census.ts` holds both
the writer and the reader, following the same convention as PIX1 and PCB1. `mailwoman gazetteer
census` builds the census from the WOF admin DB through the projection table above. The table is
executable as `PLACETYPE_PROJECTION` in `mailwoman/gazetteer-pipeline/placetype-census.ts`, so a
placetype missing from the table makes the build throw instead of silently going uncounted. The GB
artifact has 5,577 nodes (137 KB) over 33,899 counted child links.

The offline probe measured the following, with these limits:

- A census hit is **real conditional evidence**. Of GB's 16,987 distinct locality-class surfaces,
  33.2% have any dependent-locality child, so a hit rules out two thirds of the parent population.
  Against PPD post towns (25.7M rows, a source the census never read), **74.3% of rows** land in a
  covered town, and 77.5% of the rows that carry a dependent locality do.
- The complementary measurement concerns the pair index. It resolves **11.6%** of WOF's 22,843 GB
  dependent-locality links. The long tail makes up most of the space, and the census is designed to
  cover it.
- **The census is parent-scoped and cannot do span-level work.** On the 56-row law-1 confound
  board, where directional `neighbourhood` surfaces begin venue names, every row shares the London
  parent with the true positives. The census therefore has dependent-locality mass on 100% of them.
  A census bias alone would raise the venue span exactly as much as the real dependent locality.
  This is a limit of the artifact, and tuning cannot remove it.

The census therefore ships **without a `delta`**. The header field exists and is optional, and it
stays unset until a calibration step combines the census with span-level evidence and measures a
magnitude. The share within a node turned out to carry no information, because WOF rarely parents a
locality under a locality, so covered nodes read ~100% dependent-locality everywhere. The useful
statistics are presence and magnitude across the parent population.

**The observability step (2026-08-05).** The census is now loaded and probed at runtime, and it
still applies no bias. `resolvePlacetypeCensusPath` (`neural/weights.ts`) finds
`placetype-census-<cc>.bin` at the build-local data root. The file is deliberately left out of the
weights tarballs, where it would add 137–165 KB per locale for an artifact that no code reads back.
The placetype-pair prior probes the census for the parent half of every (child, parent) candidate
it tries, using the same fold. The result is a `placetypeCensus` record on the parse trace
(`{parent, childTagsPresent, lift}` per known parent, plus the probed-parent count as the
denominator), and its `applied` field is hard-coded to `false`. The decode is byte-identical with
or without the census, and `neural/placetype-census-observability.test.ts` asserts this at both the
prior level and the full-decode level. That test defines the interface: a calibration δ must be
measured from these traces and cannot be added without that measurement.

The first real traces showed two things. `shoreditch, london` (en-GB) reads `london` at lift 1.52×
on `dependent_locality`, so the evidence is present and points in the right direction.
`astoria, queens, ny` (en-US) records **zero** observations over three probed parents. `queens` has
no census node, because `PARENT_PLACETYPES` is `["locality", "localadmin"]` and Queens is a
`borough`. The US pair index's characteristic edge, a dependent locality under a borough, is the
case PIX2 was built to express. That edge is currently outside the census's parent vocabulary. A
calibration step that reads only GB traces will not detect this. (Re-measured on the post-rebuild
gazetteer, 2026-08-05: GB 5,577 nodes / 136,675 bytes / 33,936 links; US 7,532 nodes / 164,764
bytes / 123,909 links.)

The census does not become more pair entries for the following reason. A pair entry _asserts_ that
a surface is a dependent locality, so each batch needs a venue-confound board before it ships. A
census node makes no statement about any surface. It can only shift a reading that the model already
considers, under a parent the model already identified. That property makes it safe for covering a
tail that a batch-by-batch board process would never finish.

## The doctrine constraints (standing, non-negotiable)

1. **Use positive evidence only.** Presence boosts a tag, and absence stays neutral. A subtree
   without boroughs must never _forbid_ borough, because absence from the gazetteer can reflect
   coverage rather than fact (WOF Japan is point-geometry; see the meaning-of-zero rule).
2. **Bias, never mask.** The model decides segmentation, and the hierarchy contributes soft
   evidence. Hard matching produces venue-confound false positives (window mode measured 52% FP,
   and the constrained segment mode keeps the shipped floor below 2%).
3. **Per-locale conventions still decide expectations.** One model serves every locale, and each
   locale's conventions (codex address-system conventions) govern what it _expects_ to emit.
4. **Locale-scoped artifacts must be provably inert elsewhere.** en-us ships no pair index, and a
   dedicated test asserts that GB-shaped input receives no pair bias through en-us. That test is a
   packaging safeguard rather than doctrine. The US joins the census program through its own instances,
   starting with boroughs. The other direction was measured on 2026-08-01 too: an experimental US
   index misses all five GB canonical pairs (Shoreditch/London, Nine Elms/London, Clapham/London,
   Camden/London, Didsbury/Manchester). The browser's `us` fallback for postcode-less queries would
   therefore not produce GB bias if a US artifact shipped.

## Why en-us has no pair index today (and why that's sequencing rather than doctrine)

The GB/NZ indexes exist because those postal formats carry a dependent-locality or suburb line,
and national registers (HM Land Registry PPD, LINZ) provide millions of pair rows. The US format has
no such line, because USPS routes by city/state/ZIP. The US therefore has no postal register to
harvest, and mail delivery does not depend on the line. The schema's umbrella term still means that
US dependent localities exist (NYC's five boroughs), and WOF supplies the pairs directly (211
boroughs with parents, globally).

**Corrected 2026-08-01 (campaign R5).** This section previously said the US instance depended on
"the tag's _contextual_ aliveness for US". That claim was wrong for the US and for GB. Three
measurements show why, in the order that determines the result:

1. **No mechanism suppresses the tag per country.** The decoder has exactly one hard emission mask
   (`-1e9`), and it has a single row: FR `street_suffix`. `dependent_locality` appears in no
   `forbiddenTags` table, in TS or Python. `componentsSupported` omits the tag for en-US, but its
   only consumer validates the policy registry, and no code reads it to mask emissions.
2. **The model almost never predicts the tag, in every locale equally.** Raw pre-prior logits rank
   `B-dependent_locality` 11th–15th of 25 in every locale, including the GB inputs that parse
   correctly today (`Shoreditch` 0.14%, rank 14, against `locality` at 31.3%). US surfaces have
   _more_ raw mass than the GB surfaces that work. The en-GB model card had already measured the
   deficit as "large but UNIFORM (~7.0 logits mean)". It comes from a 0.3 class weight on
   `B/I-dependent_locality` used from v0.5.1 through v0.8.0. The weight has since been corrected to
   1.0, but the shipped lineage has not been retrained with it.
3. **GB emits the tag because an artifact overcomes a deficit that applies in every locale.** A US pair index built from WOF (49,033 pairs, with `borough`
   admitted as a parent placetype because WOF parents US `neighbourhood` rows to the locality
   rather than the borough) makes the US behave exactly like GB.
   `123 Main St, Park Slope, Brooklyn, NY 11215` changes from `locality=Park Slope` with
   **"Brooklyn" silently dropped** to `dependent_locality=Park Slope, locality=Brooklyn`. The
   gauntlet is unchanged, the venue-confound board has 0/60 false positives, and a positive board
   is 60/60 tag-correct.

The US instance therefore depends on shipping an artifact rather than on training, and the same
holds for every other locale in the campaign's wave 2–4 list. Whether `pair-index-us.bin`
ships inside the flagship en-us package is a packaging and defaults decision, because it changes
US parse output for every consumer. It is not a question about the model.

## Coverage boundaries measured so far (2026-08-01 probe set)

The GB pair source (PPD) covers England and Wales and leaves the city field empty for London. By
the source's own construction, London `neighbourhood` rows, estates, Northern Ireland and the
Republic all fell outside the shipped index. **Great Britain is now covered end to end**, entirely
from open data:

| rung     | source                                        | pairs      |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| baseline | HM Land Registry PPD                          | 19,209     |
| R2       | WOF boroughs                                  | 15         |
| R3 / R4b | ONSPD wards + WOF London `neighbourhood` rows | 815        |
| R7       | WOF Northern Ireland                          | 87         |
| R8       | WOF Scotland, Wales, England                  | 10,708     |
|          | **GB total, cross-checked**                   | **30,834** |

Every increment after the baseline passed a venue-confound board before shipping, and no board has
recorded a false positive.

**Northern Ireland's blocker turned out not to exist.** Its pairs were deferred for months on the premise
that "the pair parent needs post towns", which requires a licensed Royal Mail table. That premise
came from the shape of GB's source (PPD's parent column happens to be the post town), not from what
a pair requires. WOF parents NI `neighbourhood` rows to Belfast, Newtownabbey, Londonderry and
Lisburn, which _are_ those post towns. When a premise changes, re-check any blocker that depended
on it.

The GB work had to make two differences between register spellings and written addresses
explicit. Both apply generally:

- A slash-separated WOF parent (`Londonderry / Derry`) is an **alias set** rather than a name. It folds to
  one key that matches neither alternative, so such pairs ship in the artifact but can never fire.
  Emit one pair per alternative.
- A civil-parish suffix (`Pontypridd Community`, `Llanelli Rural`) is part of the administrative
  name only. The register uses that form for the parish, but an address says `Pontypridd`. Strip
  the suffix instead of dropping the pair, which would have discarded 1,545 Welsh pairs.

**Every locale on the original wave 2–4 list has now shipped.** Data availability was never the
blocker. The pair index only fires for the resolved locale's country, so an artifact inside another
locale's package can never fire. Each locale needed its own **carrier package**, a ~6-file
data-only overlay that declares `baseWeights`. After that requirement was understood, the
remaining work was routine.

| locale |   pairs | source       | note                                                  |
| ------ | ------: | ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| GB     |  30,825 | PPD + WOF    | England, Scotland, Wales, NI, London                  |
| US     |  47,878 | WOF          | no postal register carries the line                   |

**Ireland is the only locale left, and it is the only one ever blocked by something other than
packaging work.** Its sources (Tailte Éireann, logainm) need a license survey before anything can
be harvested.

The non-GB instances established three things that the GB work never encountered:

- **The postal format determines the source.** France's lieu-dit comes from BAN because it _is_
  the postal line. The US, DE, ES, IT and IN use WOF because no postal register carries their
  sub-city line.
- **A leading postcode makes the artifact inert.** France, Germany, Spain and Italy write
  `12210 Montpeyroux`, so a parent segment folds to a key that no bare-locality entry matches. Each
  country needed a `LEADING_POSTCODE_COUNTRIES` entry. In every case, the artifact built correctly
  but had no effect until that entry was added.
- **WOF's preferred name is not always the written one.** WOF stores `Bangalore` for a city written
  `Bengaluru` and `Rome` for one written `Roma`. The fold does not strip accents, so `Cordoba`
  never matches `Córdoba`. Parent-alias expansion draws from names in the country's official
  languages plus English, restricted to Latin script.
