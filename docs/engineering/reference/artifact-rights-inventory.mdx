# Artifact and rights inventory

What each published package ships, which party grants rights to it, and where the record is
incomplete. Observed on `main` on 2026-09-20 by reading manifests, model cards and rights documents
in the checkout.

Three records are kept apart throughout, because conflating them is how a corpus measurement becomes
a claim about a released model:

1. **Current corpus composition** — what the corpus on the training volume contains today.
2. **Historical training inputs** — which records trained a particular released checkpoint.
3. **Runtime datasets and dependencies** — what an operator downloads separately.

Only the first is measured here. The second is unresolved for every published package, and this
document states that rather than implying otherwise.

The licensor and the services operator are different parties, and the repository states both
consistently. Nirrus LLC appears in the website Terms and Privacy Policy and in no rights document.
Operating the services does not make it the copyright holder.

Two commercial documents exist and each says which it is. `COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md` opens with a
template notice stating it does not execute. `commercial-2026-10.mdx` states that acceptance is
recorded by Stripe Checkout when a buyer ticks the box, and that the page is not edited after
publication.

**Inconsistency to repair:** `docs/src/pages/license.mdx` line 32 says `COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md`
"carries the same terms in the repository". The two differ in execution mechanism, so the page
overstates their relationship. The repair belongs on the page. Neither agreement should be edited.

**Already correct, and worth propagating:** `COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md` line 31 already limits the
grant's scope.

> This grant covers only the code and model artifacts authored by Licensor. It does not extend to
> third-party components bundled with Mailwoman … Any attribution, share-alike, or other obligations
> attaching to those third-party components continue to apply and are not waived or relicensed by
> this Agreement.

The public license page carries no equivalent sentence.

### Four packages omit the commercial branch

`es-es`, `it-it`, `ja-jp` and `zh-cn` declare `AGPL-3.0`. The other nine, and every other workspace
in the repository including `@mailwoman/core` and `@mailwoman/codex`, declare
`AGPL-3.0-only OR LicenseRef-Commercial`.

Two defects in one field. `AGPL-3.0` is a deprecated SPDX identifier, superseded by `AGPL-3.0-only`
and `AGPL-3.0-or-later`, so it does not state which. And `docs/src/pages/license.mdx` line 11 says
"every release ships under both", which those four manifests contradict.

### Twelve model cards disagree with their manifest

Every published package's model-card version differs from its manifest version, `fr-fr` most widely
at 6.5.0 against 10.0.0.

A package version and a model version describe different things, and an overlay's card describes an
inherited model rather than its own. Neither field says which it is, so a reader cannot tell a
deliberate difference from drift. The repair is to state the meaning of each field and leave the
values free to differ where they describe different things.

### Artifact digests are recorded for 6 of about 100 shipped artifacts

`en-us` records four in `files_md5` — `model.onnx`, `tokenizer.model`,
`street-type-lexicon-v3.json`, `locality-surface-lexicon-v7.json` — of fourteen declared artifacts.
`cjk` records two. The remaining eleven packages record none.

`en-us` ships artifacts with separate lineages: a trained model, a SentencePiece tokenizer, two
lexicon files, a US postcode binary, a placetype-pair index built from the Who's On First admin
hierarchy, and two FST binaries. One `data_attribution` list covers the package.

### One package carries training attribution

`en-us` has ten `data_attribution` entries. The other twelve have none, `cjk` included, which ships
its own `model.onnx` rather than inheriting one.

No package ships a `LICENSE.md`.

## Corpus composition, measured

Full scans of `v0.32.0-locality-shape` on the training volume, 2026-09-20. **This is record 1. It
describes the corpus on the volume today and states nothing about which records trained a released
checkpoint.**

| locale |  train rows | `wof-*` rows | share | `overture` |
| ------ | ----------: | -----------: | ----: | ---------: |
| GB     |  15,644,707 |   14,812,000 | 94.7% |          0 |
| DE     |   2,891,912 |    2,503,713 | 86.6% |    150,000 |
| FR     | 149,866,617 |    4,233,176 |  2.8% |          0 |

Who's On First supplies most rows for GB and DE in this corpus.

**What this does not establish.** It does not show that any OSM-derived record is present, because
Who's On First is mixed-provenance and our own notices say it draws on several sources. It does not
show that any of these rows trained a published checkpoint. It does not classify a `wof-*` row as
ODbL, and it does not classify one as unrestricted.

Resolving it needs record-level lineage on the training extracts: the source files, their lineage
fields and license metadata, and the transformations applied. Where lineage cannot be recovered, the
correct record is unresolved.

## Publication control

`packages/corpus/lib/utils/license.ts` states the posture:

> exclusion is a deliberate act rather than a silent default. The build includes every row an
> adapter yields … Nothing is dropped on a license string unless the operator named it.

`build.ts` line 238 is `const excludeLicenses = opts.excludeLicenses ?? []`, and no caller in the
repository passes it. `--exclude-share-alike` is an opt-in build flag.

The flag is neither necessary nor sufficient as evidence about a released model. It does not record
which sources contributed, and passing it does not establish that an indirectly included or
misclassified source was caught. A release control needs a reproducible source manifest and an
explicit eligibility decision per input, including inherited checkpoints and auxiliary artifacts.
