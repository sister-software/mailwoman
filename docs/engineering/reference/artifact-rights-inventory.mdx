# Artifact and rights inventory

What each published package ships, which party grants rights to it, and where the record is
incomplete. Observed on `main` on 2026-09-20 by reading manifests, model cards and rights documents
in the checkout.

Three records are kept apart throughout, because conflating them is how a corpus measurement becomes
a claim about a released model:

1. **Current corpus composition** — what the corpus on the training volume contains today.
2. **Historical training inputs** — which records trained a particular released checkpoint.
3. **Runtime datasets and dependencies** — what an operator downloads separately.

Only the first is measured here. The second is unresolved for every published package, and this
document states that rather than implying otherwise.

| Services operator   | Nirrus LLC, a Delaware limited liability company | `docs/src/pages/privacy/index.mdx`, `docs/src/pages/terms-of-service/index.mdx` |

The licensor and the services operator are different parties, and the repository states both
consistently. Nirrus LLC appears in the website Terms and Privacy Policy and in no rights document.
Operating the services does not make it the copyright holder.

Two commercial documents exist and each says which it is. `COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md` opens with a
template notice stating it does not execute. `commercial-2026-10.mdx` states that acceptance is
recorded by Stripe Checkout when a buyer ticks the box, and that the page is not edited after
publication.

**Repaired on the page.** `docs/src/pages/license.mdx` said `COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md` "carries the same
terms in the repository". The two differ in execution mechanism, so the page overstated their
relationship. The page now names `commercial-2026-10` as the agreement that binds and describes
`COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md` as the reference template its own opening notice says it is. Neither
agreement was edited.

**Already correct, and now propagated:** `COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md` line 31 limits the grant's scope.

> This grant covers only the code and model artifacts authored by Licensor. It does not extend to
> third-party components bundled with Mailwoman … Any attribution, share-alike, or other obligations
> attaching to those third-party components continue to apply and are not waived or relicensed by
> this Agreement.

The public license page carried no equivalent sentence. It now carries a section stating that the
commercial grant covers the code and model artifacts Sister Software authors, that third-party
components stay under their own licenses, that attribution and share-alike obligations attaching to
them continue to apply, and that separately downloaded reference data carries the terms of whoever
published it.

### Four packages omit the commercial branch

`es-es`, `it-it`, `ja-jp` and `zh-cn` declare `AGPL-3.0`. The other nine, and every other workspace
in the repository including `@mailwoman/core` and `@mailwoman/codex`, declare
`AGPL-3.0-only OR LicenseRef-Commercial`.

Two defects in one field. `AGPL-3.0` is a deprecated SPDX identifier, superseded by `AGPL-3.0-only`
and `AGPL-3.0-or-later`, so it does not state which. And `docs/src/pages/license.mdx` line 11 says
"every release ships under both", which those four manifests contradict.

The repository's own admissibility function already refuses the identifier. Measured on this tree:
`assertAdmissibleLicenseExpression("AGPL-3.0")` throws, and `summarizeLicense("AGPL-3.0")` returns
`recognized: false` with `unrecognized: ["AGPL-3.0"]` and an empty obligation list. That function
guarded spatial-layer manifests and nothing had applied it to a package manifest.

**Repaired.** The four fields now read `AGPL-3.0-only OR LicenseRef-Commercial`, `docs/package.json`
gained the field it lacked, and the `package-license` repository check refuses a workspace manifest
that declares no license, declares an identifier the obligations table does not know, or declares an
expression other than the root's. The four packages have not been republished, so the versions on npm
still carry the old field.

### Twelve model cards carry a version their manifest does not, and the card says which series it is

Every published package's model-card version differs from its manifest version, `fr-fr` most widely
at 6.5.0 against 10.0.0.

**Correcting an earlier reading in this document.** The first version of this section called all
twelve a release-engineering defect. For the graph packages that reading is wrong. Two version series
exist by design and `docs/records/site-2026-08/releases.mdx` states them, with the current row reading
`**10.0.0** (current) · model 9.1.0 (unchanged)` — a code-only release over unchanged model bytes.
`mwops release verify-metadata` keys its three surface checks off the card version rather than the npm
version for exactly that reason, and `fetch-hf-weights` names the Hugging Face directory
`<family>/v<card version>/`. Forcing the numbers equal would break both.

What the cards did not state is which series a given number belongs to. Two packages ship a model
graph: `en-us` at card 9.1.0 and `cjk` at card 0.0.3. Ten are data-only overlays declaring
`mailwoman.baseWeights`, and an overlay's card version names that overlay's own artifacts while the
graph its rows decode through is the base package's. Nothing in a published tarball said which of the
two a reader was holding.

**Repaired.** Each published package's generated `PROVENANCE.json` carries `version_series`, derived
from `mailwoman.baseWeights` rather than asserted, together with a sentence stating what that series
means and how it relates to `package_version`. The numbers are left free to differ.

### Artifact digests are recorded for 6 of about 100 shipped artifacts

`en-us` records four in `files_md5` — `model.onnx`, `tokenizer.model`,
`street-type-lexicon-v3.json`, `locality-surface-lexicon-v7.json` — of fourteen declared artifacts.
`cjk` records two. The remaining eleven packages record none.

`en-us` ships artifacts with separate lineages: a trained model, a SentencePiece tokenizer, two
lexicon files, a US postcode binary, a placetype-pair index built from the Who's On First admin
hierarchy, and two FST binaries. One `data_attribution` list covers the package.

### One package carries training attribution

`en-us` has ten `data_attribution` entries. The other twelve have none, `cjk` included, which ships
its own `model.onnx` rather than inheriting one.

One of those ten entries attributes an artifact that ships in a different package. Entry 1 names
`pair-index-gb.bin` in `neural-weights-en-gb`, attributing it to HM Land Registry Price Paid Data
under OGL v3.0 with Crown copyright and attribution required. `neural-weights-en-gb`'s own card has no
`training` section at all. A consumer who installs the overlay alone receives the binary and no
attribution.

No package shipped a `LICENSE.md`.

**Repaired.** Every published weights package now carries two generated files, written by
`mwops release write-rights-files` and held current by the `weights-rights` repository check.
`LICENSE.md` states the terms this repository grants, that the commercial grant covers only the code
and model artifacts Sister Software authors, and that third-party obligations continue to apply.
`PROVENANCE.json` records each declared artifact with its digest or `unrecorded`, each attribution
entry with the license it names or `null`, and an `unresolved` list. A package whose card records no
attribution reads `none-recorded-in-this-package` rather than reading as a package with nothing to
attribute. The cross-package case above appears in `en-gb`'s record under
`attribution_recorded_in_another_package`, found by matching each package's artifact filenames against
every other package's attribution text rather than by hand.

Generating the files does not discharge the obligation. `pair-index-gb.bin`'s attribution is still
recorded in another package's card, and the repair for that is to put the entry where the artifact
ships.

## Corpus composition, measured

Full scans of `v0.32.0-locality-shape` on the training volume, 2026-09-20. **This is record 1. It
describes the corpus on the volume today and states nothing about which records trained a released
checkpoint.**

| locale |  train rows | `wof-*` rows | share | `overture` |
| ------ | ----------: | -----------: | ----: | ---------: |
| GB     |  15,644,707 |   14,812,000 | 94.7% |          0 |
| DE     |   2,891,912 |    2,503,713 | 86.6% |    150,000 |
| FR     | 149,866,617 |    4,233,176 |  2.8% |          0 |

Who's On First supplies most rows for GB and DE in this corpus.

**What this does not establish.** It does not show that any OSM-derived record is present, because
Who's On First is mixed-provenance and our own notices say it draws on several sources. It does not
show that any of these rows trained a published checkpoint. It does not classify a `wof-*` row as
ODbL, and it does not classify one as unrestricted.

Resolving it needs record-level lineage on the training extracts: the source files, their lineage
fields and license metadata, and the transformations applied. Where lineage cannot be recovered, the
correct record is unresolved.

## Publication control

`packages/corpus/lib/utils/license.ts` states the posture:

> exclusion is a deliberate act rather than a silent default. The build includes every row an
> adapter yields … Nothing is dropped on a license string unless the operator named it.

`build.ts` line 238 is `const excludeLicenses = opts.excludeLicenses ?? []`, and no caller in the
repository passes it. `--exclude-share-alike` is an opt-in build flag.

The flag is neither necessary nor sufficient as evidence about a released model. It does not record
which sources contributed, and passing it does not establish that an indirectly included or
misclassified source was caught. A release control needs a reproducible source manifest and an
explicit eligibility decision per input, including inherited checkpoints and auxiliary artifacts.

### The one workspace held for a rights reason had no enforced hold

`packages/osm` is held out of the release pending ODbL counsel sign-off. It is not `private`, and it
declares `publishConfig.access: public`. The hold was membership in `SANCTIONED_RELEASE_ABSENCES` in
`packages/release-kit/lib/release/stage.ts`, a record that `checkReleaseListIdentity` reads so a
missing workspace fails with its name. Nothing on the publish path read it. Running
`mwops release publish-workspace --allow-unplanned` against that directory, or `npm publish` by
hand, would have published it.

**Repaired.** `assertWorkspacePublishable` refuses any workspace the record names, quoting the
recorded reason, and both publish paths call it: `publishWorkspace` before it packs, and
`blessPackages` across every named directory before the first publish. Publishing a held-out
workspace now means deleting its entry, which a reviewer sees in the diff. Every entry is refused
rather than the rights-held ones alone, so one rule cannot drift from a classification it does not
carry.

**What this control does not do.** It refuses a workspace by name. It does not decide whether an
artifact's inputs permit publication, and it does not read a provenance record, because no frozen
training manifest exists to read.

### A model upload states its training sources or it does not go out

`mailwoman release hf` in `packages/mailwoman/lib/release-tools/publish-hf.ts` uploads `model.onnx`,
`tokenizer.model`, `model-card.json` and the FST and postcode binaries. It read those files' sizes and
nothing else about them. Uploading is publication, and attribution and share-alike conditions attach
to a source and survive redistribution, so the moment to establish that a record exists is before the
bytes leave.

**Repaired.** `verifyTrainingProvenance` runs after the artifact checks and refuses a card whose
`training.data_attribution` is absent or empty, naming the card and saying to record the sources the
run trained on in it. Entries that name no
license are printed with their text rather than refused, so the operator sees which source is
unaccounted for at the moment of publication. Missing evidence about a source is a gap to record,
while a finding against a source is a conclusion somebody has to reach, and a release control is not
the place to reach it.

**Consequence to act on.** `neural-weights-cjk` carries a `training` section recording its corpus and
no `data_attribution` at all. A character-path model release is refused today until that card records
the sources the run trained on. `neural-weights-en-us` passes with ten entries, two of which name no
license: the OpenAddresses PL entry and the pointer to `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`.

| Which sources the character-path model trained on                     | `cjk`, `ja-jp`, `zh-cn`   | Its card records a corpus and no attribution       |

Each package's `PROVENANCE.json` carries the subset of these questions that bears on it, so the
record travels with the tarball rather than staying in this document.

No package in this inventory is recorded as cleared. Missing evidence is recorded as missing, and it
is neither evidence of compliance nor evidence of infringement.
