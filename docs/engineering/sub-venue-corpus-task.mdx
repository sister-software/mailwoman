# Sub-venue structure is a corpus gap, not a decode gap

`West Wing`, `North Terminal`, `Concourse B`, `Gate 12` — the interior structure of a large venue.
The span proposer finds these correctly and the decoder discards most of them, and the reason is not
a weight that needs turning up.

## What the measurements say

Two proposal shapes reach the same tag by different routes.

**Designator + identifier** (`Concourse B`, `Terminal 5`, `Gate 12`) proposes at 0.85 confidence and
needed a bias scale of 5.90 to win the decode. That shipped: `venueStructureBiasScale`, default 6.0.

**Modifier + designator** (`West Wing`, `North Terminal`) proposes at 0.6 and needs far more:

| case            | required scale |
| --------------- | -------------: |
| West Wing       |           5.87 |
| East Wing       |           8.34 |
| South Concourse |           9.04 |
| Upper Concourse |           9.65 |
| North Terminal  |      **10.65** |

Only the first clears the shipped 6.0, which is exactly the one that parses today.

Closing the class by weight would take a scale near 11 — **nearly double what the stronger evidence
needed**. A positional word before a designator is a shape ordinary street names also take; an
identifier after one is nearly unambiguous. Paying more for the weaker cue inverts the evidence
ordering, and at 11 nats a prior stops being a prior: it overrides the model wherever it fires, on
the strength of a six-row confound board.

The distribution is the tell. 5.87 is a model that half-believes; 10.65 is a model confidently
believing something else. That is the signature of a tag it was never taught, and it matches the
campaign's aliveness finding — `B-unit` ranks 11th–15th of 25 in raw pre-prior logits across every
locale, at 0.15–0.42% even for `Apt` and `Suite`.

## What is already in place

Shipped, and not to be redone:

- `VENUE_STRUCTURE_DESIGNATORS` — `arcade`, `building`, `campus`, `concourse`, `enclosure`,
  `installation`, `wing` from the WOF placetype vocabulary, plus `terminal` and `gate` from OSM's
  `aeroway` key.
- `VENUE_STRUCTURE_MODIFIERS` + `MODIFIER_ELIGIBLE_STRUCTURE_DESIGNATORS` — the positional words, and
  the narrower designator subset they may precede. `gate` and `building` are excluded because "East
  Gate" and "Building Society Place" are streets.
- Both proposal rules, with two structural guards: a preceding house number means a street line, and
  a preceding capitalized word means the pair sits inside a longer proper name ("Grand Central
  Terminal" contains "Central Terminal").
- A 30-row confound board as a gate (`fixtures/venue-structure-confounds.jsonl`).

The proposer emits the right span on 5 of 6 target shapes with zero false positives. The gap is
entirely downstream.

## The corpus task

Teach the tag instead of outvoting it. Synthesize lines of the shape

```
<sub-venue>, <venue>, <street>, <locality>, <postcode>
```

where the sub-venue carries both forms:

- designator + identifier — `Terminal 5`, `Gate 12`, `Concourse B`, `Wing B`, `Building 43`
- modifier + designator — `West Wing`, `North Terminal`, `Upper Concourse`, `Main Building`

with the sub-venue labelled `unit` and the containing venue labelled `venue`.

Include the confound shapes as NEGATIVES in the same shard, or the model learns the surface rather
than the structure: `12 East Gate` (street), `Briggate` / `Kirkgate` (single-token GB street names),
`Gate House` (venue), `Wing Yip` (business name), `Grand Central Terminal` (the pair inside a longer
name).

## Get real data first, and get a lot of it

Do not synthesize these from a template list. Airports and transit terminals are the densest real
source of sub-venue structure in existence, they are globally distributed, and the naming is
genuinely varied in ways an invented list will not be — `Concourse B`, `Terminal 2F`, `Pier C`,
`Satellite 4`, `Hall M`, `Gate A12`, `North Terminal`, `Terminal Sur`.

This is also the class of query a geocoder is expected to answer and currently answers badly. "Which
terminal" is a real user intent with a real coordinate attached.

Sources worth pulling, roughly in order of value:

- **OSM `aeroway=terminal` / `aeroway=gate`** — already the provenance for `terminal` and `gate` in
  the designator vocabulary, and `osm/sdk` already fetches and shards OSM. The extraction path
  exists; this is a new extractor over data we can already reach.
- **OSM `public_transport=station` + `railway=station`** with their platform and concourse children —
  the rail equivalent, and the naming conventions differ enough from aviation to be worth having
  both.
- **OurAirports** (public domain) — every airport worldwide with IATA/ICAO codes and coordinates.
  Good for the venue side of each pair, weaker on interior structure.
- **Overture Places** — already ingested for `poi.db`; filter to transport categories for venue names
  with coordinates.
- **Wikidata** — terminal and concourse entities carry multilingual labels, which is the cheapest
  route to non-English sub-venue naming (`Terminal Sur`, `Halle 2`, `ターミナル1`).

The interior structure is the scarce part. Airport terminal and gate data is the densest source of it
available, and the vocabulary this arc is built on already came from there — extending the same
source into the corpus is the natural next step, not a new dependency.

## Bars

Whatever the shard produces has to hold the existing board: 30 confound rows, zero `unit` emissions,
and the shipped designator+identifier cases (`Concourse B`, `Terminal 5`, `Gate 12`, `Wing B`) stay
correct. The five modifier cases above are the target; `West Wing` already passes and must not
regress.

Success is the required-scale table collapsing — the modifier class winning at the shipped 6.0
rather than needing 11. If a retrained model still needs 9+, the shard did not teach the structure
and the data is the thing to look at, not the scale.
