We spent a night trying to make our neural address parser less overconfident. The diagnostics showed a different problem: the training data contained no examples of whole categories of address.

{/* truncate */}

## The hypothesis: it's overconfident

Across the v0.6.x training cycle, one pattern kept surfacing: when the model was wrong, it was _confidently_ wrong. On a held-out test set, **86% of its incorrect predictions were made at ≥0.9 confidence**, and most of those at a flat 1.00. We reasoned that a model that hedged appropriately would stop overriding good answers with bad high-confidence ones.

**Label smoothing** is the standard tool for that. Instead of training toward a one-hot target (1.0 for the right tag, 0 for the rest), you train toward a softer target (0.9 for the right tag, with the rest spread across the others). This caps how peaked the model's outputs can get. We ran a single-variable experiment (the v0.6.0 recipe plus `label_smoothing=0.1`, with no other variable changed) and measured.

Label smoothing did what it is designed to do. Overconfidence-on-wrong dropped **86% → 67%**, and the mass at 1.00 confidence disappeared, with confidence capped around 0.95. Postcode recall rose slightly.

The metric we ship on, **harness pass rate**, didn't improve. It went from 14.6% to 13.8%. Two tags (house numbers and streets) _regressed_.

## The evidence

A better-calibrated model that performs no better at the task needed explaining. Instead of tuning the smoothing value again, we asked a more direct question: **of everything the harness gets wrong, what kind of error is it?**

We categorized every failure:

- **55% of the gap was missing labels.** The model emitted _no tag at all_ where one belonged, rather than a wrong value or a fuzzy boundary.
- The most-missed tags were `street` (×197) and `house_number` (×100).
- One cluster stood out: **intersections**: addresses like `Broadway & W 42nd St`. They're 17% of our harness, and the model scored **0%** on them.

Calibration softens the confidence of labels the model _does_ emit. It cannot produce a label the model never emits, so the harness pass rate stayed flat.

## The probe

We ran a single probe on a canonical intersection. For every token in `Broadway & W 42nd St`, we read off the probability the model assigned to the `intersection_a` / `intersection_b` tags. The maximum across every token was **~0.0001**.

An uncertain model still puts _some_ probability on the right tag. A maximum of ~0.0001 means the model has _no representation of intersections_. The labels existed in its output vocabulary, but it had never learned to use them.

We checked the corpus pipeline. It has synthesizers for streets, venues without a street, PO boxes, and house+venue combinations, and **no synthesizer that generates intersections**. The real-world adapters don't emit them in that form either.

## Alphanumeric postcodes and the regex repair

The one improvement from calibration (a small postcode gain) pointed at a _second_ coverage gap, this one caused by tokenization.

The subword tokenizer splits alphanumeric postcodes (`SW1A 1AA`, `M5V 2T6`) into fragments like `["S","##W","##1","##A", "1","##AA"]`. A regex recognizes the seven-character shape easily, but the model sees only disconnected pieces. As a result, GB/CA/NL postcodes scored 0%.

This fix needed no training. A **deterministic regex repair** runs _after_ the model decodes. It detects a postcode-shaped substring and aligns the label span to it. On the postcode harness that single pass fixed **135 cases and regressed zero**, taking GB/CA/DE/PT to 100%. The repair is eight lines of pattern matching plus a "longest-match-wins" rule that keeps a US ZIP+4 from matching as a Dutch postcode.

## Metrics, coverage and structural checks

- **Pick a metric that the change you're testing cannot inflate on its own.** Per-tag F1 looked fine while the product was stuck. Harness pass rate asks whether the _whole_ address comes out right, and it showed the real state.
- **A confidently wrong model and a model missing training data need different fixes.** We assumed the first case, and the data showed the second.
- **Structural validity is a separate signal.** A checker that flags incoherent parses, such as a house number without a street or an orphaned unit, caught a mid-training regression that the headline accuracy number hid.
- **A model cannot learn a category that's absent from its training data.** The problem looked expensive, and its cause, a missing synthesizer, was the cheapest one to fix.

The fix for intersections is a couple thousand synthetic `X & Y St` examples, labeled and added to the corpus as a small targeted supplement, plus a retrain. That run is training as we publish this.

We'll report the model's intersection results after that run finishes.
