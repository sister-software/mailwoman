Mailwoman ships two address parsers in the same box.

We call the first one **v0**. It is our TypeScript port of the [Pelias parser](https://github.com/pelias/parser), a rules engine: tokenize, classify each token against dictionaries and patterns, solve for the most plausible arrangement under a pile of hand-written constraints. It is fast and deterministic, it encodes years of accumulated postal knowledge, and it is the baseline we set out to beat.

The second is the **neural** classifier, a sequence labeler trained on a BIO-tagged corpus, the one this blog has spent most of its life arguing with.

A year in, did the neural net beat the rules parser? On clean US addresses the two are close to a tie. On PO boxes, intersections and units, the neural model is ahead.

## The scoreboard

We don't grade the two parsers against each other's labels, because that measures which one looks more like the other. We grade both through the **same resolver**, against real address points: take 2,000 [OpenAddresses](https://openaddresses.io/) US rows the model never trained on, parse each one, resolve it to a coordinate, and measure how frequently we land in the right city, the right state, and how far the resolved point is from the truth.

| parser           | locality match | region match | coord p50 |   coord p90 |  coord p99 |
| ---------------- | -------------: | -----------: | --------: | ----------: | ---------: |
| **neural**       |      **84.0%** |    **99.9%** |    3.3 km | **10.7 km** | **239 km** |
| v0 (Pelias port) |          82.1% |        99.5% |    3.3 km |     11.2 km |     277 km |

On clean US addresses it is close. Neural is a couple of points ahead on locality, a hair ahead on region, dead even at the median coordinate, and the difference you can feel is in the **tail**: neural's p99 error is 239 km versus 277. Both parsers nail the easy ones. The neural net throws fewer wild misses.

(Pelias publishes architecture docs and no accuracy numbers of its own. That's exactly why v0 _is_ our baseline. We can't compare to a number the Pelias project never printed, so we ported the thing and graded it ourselves.)

## Where the neural net pulls ahead

Here is the same handful of addresses through both parsers. Watch the rules engine on anything that isn't `<number> <street>, <city>, <state> <zip>`.

**A PO box**, the classic case. `PO Box 1207, Anchorage, AK 99510`:

```
v0    : street="PO Box"  house_number=1207  locality=Anchorage  region=AK  postcode=99510
neural: po_box="PO Box 1207"  locality=Anchorage  region=AK  postcode=99510
```

The rules parser reads "PO Box 1207" as a street called "PO Box" with house number 1207. By its own rules that parse is consistent: "Box" looks like a street type and a number follows it. It has no concept of a _post office box_, a different kind of place entirely. The neural net learned the `po_box` tag and uses it.

**An intersection**: `5th & Main, Springfield, IL`:

```
v0    : street="5th"  locality=Springfield  region=IL
neural: intersection_a="5th"  intersection_b="Main"  locality=Springfield  region=IL
```

v0 keeps the first street and **drops "Main" on the floor**. The neural net knows an intersection has two sides and labels both.

**A unit**: `1600 Pennsylvania Ave NW Apt 4B`:

```
v0    : house_number=1600  street="Pennsylvania Ave NW"  unit="4B"  ...
neural: house_number=1600  street="Pennsylvania Ave NW"  unit="Apt 4B"  ...
```

Both find the unit. The neural net keeps the designator ("Apt") attached, where v0 strips it to the bare "4B".

The neural model has these tags because we trained it on examples that contain them, a discipline we've written about before as **negative space**: you teach the model the tags the rules engine never had. From our parity evals, `unit` coverage went from **0 → 92%**, `street_prefix` 0 → 78%, `street_suffix` 0 → 67%, components the Pelias port doesn't emit. And on multi-locale evals the same recipe is why native-order German resolves locality at ~90% where a US-order assumption falls to the 40s (we've told that story before, in "the right name in the wrong state").

On the addresses that fit the template, the parsers tie. On PO boxes, intersections, units, sub-street components, and non-US word order, the neural net emits tags the rules engine doesn't have.

## Where we still struggle (and what we haven't touched)

- **Clean addresses are a tie rather than a win.** The two parsers above agree on `350 5th Ave` down to the components: neural splits `street="5th"` + `street_suffix="Ave"` where v0 keeps `"5th Ave"` whole, and _both are correct_. For a model this good on US structure, matching the rules engine is the target on clean addresses.
- **Reordered house numbers.** French addresses that lead with the postcode still trip the model: `fr.house_number` plateaus around 87% on the hardest reordered stratum, where the model assigns the leading number to `house_number`. We pushed it from 54% to 87% and then hit a wall that a training-weight tweak won't move. The next change is real reordered data rather than more synthetic.
- **Whole locales we haven't tackled.** Japanese, Korean, and Taiwanese addressing — block-and-lot systems that don't map onto a Western street grid — are scoped but unbuilt. So is the last stubborn 0% class in the postal arena: Commonwealth and military PO-box formats (BFPO, APO/FPO).
- **Coordinate precision.** Today's US coordinate is the admin-centroid tier: we land you in the right city, and "right city" is a centroid that can sit tens of kilometers from an edge address. The finer tiers (postcode centroid, then street-level interpolation) are wired but not yet the default everywhere.

## We even tried to have both

If the neural net wins on weird addresses and ties on clean ones, the obvious move is to run both parsers and keep the better answer per component. We built a per-component arbitration layer to do that.

On our label-match arena, arbitration captured 122 parses that the neural net by itself "missed." Then we graded the **assembled coordinate output** instead of the labels: locality match fell from 83% to 57%, the median coordinate error grew from 3 km to **over a thousand**, and half the addresses lost their street-and-number entirely.

The arena's "neural missed it" column conflates two very different things: addresses the neural net gets _wrong_, and addresses it gets _differently right_. When v0 writes `street="Seminary Dr"` and neural writes `street="Seminary"` + `street_suffix="Dr"`, arbitrating toward v0 "wins" on label-match and **loses** on the actual parse, and flattening the two parsers' outputs together destroyed the tree structure the resolver needs to tell one "Mill Valley" from another. We rebuilt the layer to preserve that structure. The regression vanished, and so did the result. The safe version is a net wash.

For a model this strong on the addresses we serve, **blending it with the rules parser left coordinate accuracy where the neural model put it without the blend, once the blend kept the tree intact**. Where neural differs from v0 it is differently right rather than wrong, and a differently-right parse isn't something to overwrite. We grade the coordinate now, never the label, and we let the arbitration implementation sit behind a default-off flag for the day a weaker model or a new locale makes it pay.

## What's on deck

The parser is at or ahead of a mature rules engine on clean US addresses and ahead on the hard structured tags, and the gaps are listed above. The next moves are about feeding it and placing it more precisely:

- **Real data over synthetic.** Overture Maps address ingestion, to widen the corpus with real reordered, multi-locale, and long-tail formats, the thing the fr-house-number wall told us we need.
- **Finer coordinate tiers.** Make postcode-centroid and street-level interpolation the default where the data supports it, so "right city" becomes "right block."
- **Geocode-first record matching.** The other half of the box: once every record resolves to a coordinate, you can match records by where they are rather than how they're spelled. That's the entity-resolution layer we've been building on top of all of this, and the subject of its own recent post.
