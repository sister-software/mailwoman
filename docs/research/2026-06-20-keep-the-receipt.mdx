Every geocoder turns an address into a coordinate. Almost none of them will tell you where that coordinate came from. The latitude, longitude, and confidence category leave you unable to tell whether a wrong point came from a federal release, a county GIS office, or street interpolation. Mailwoman keeps the source on every point. Here's New York, every dot colored by the open dataset it came from.

![Address points across New York, each colored by its source dataset: green for the federal National Address Database statewide, orange for OpenAddresses (the city's own NYC Open Data) concentrated in New York City.](/img/address-provenance-ny.png)

Green is the National Address Database. Orange is OpenAddresses. For New York, OpenAddresses republishes the city's own NYC Open Data. You can [explore the interactive version](pathname:///maps/address-provenance-ny.html) and click any dot to see its publisher.

## Federal data across the state and city data in New York City

The federal release blankets the state. New York City has its own color because OpenAddresses republishes the city's own address file. No guess was made about which is which. Every point kept its source, from ingest all the way to this map. The method is a `source` column that no pipeline stage dropped.

## The datasets behind the points

None of this is our data. The National Address Database is a federal release. OpenAddresses is a decade of county and municipal publishers doing the unglamorous work of putting their address files online. The street network underneath is Census TIGER. All three are free, open and good, and the lookup is the easy part of geocoding. Each pipeline must assemble these sources, keep them fresh, and preserve their provenance. That record lets a user trace a wrong coordinate to the dataset and release that produced it.

## The layers under the dots

This map shows the rooftop-point sources. Where there's no rooftop point, the geocoder falls back to TIGER street interpolation, a position estimated along the block, and below that to an administrative centroid from Who's On First. The coordinate records which open dataset supplied it. That lets you distinguish a Manhattan rooftop point from a position estimated along a street in a county with sparse coverage.

## The interactive map and the source column

[Explore the interactive map](pathname:///maps/address-provenance-ny.html) — serve it over localhost, since the basemap tiles are CORS-scoped to the house domains. The provenance is a plain column on the address-point table:

```sql
SELECT lat, lon, source FROM address_point WHERE locality_norm = 'brooklyn' LIMIT 1;
-- 40.6782 | -73.9442 | overture:OpenAddresses/NY/NYC Open Data
```

Point the same map at any state:

```sh
node \
  scripts/record-matcher/viz/source-provenance-map.ts --state ny
```
