# Why one building can have several useful addresses

Why can two address records point to the same building but require different delivery details? A building coordinate identifies a location. An address can instead identify a unit, a mailbox, a recipient, or a postal route. The right check depends on what the application needs the address to do.

## A unit distinguishes destinations inside a building

The United States Postal Service [secondary-unit guidance](https://pe.usps.com/text/pub28/pub28c2_003.htm) requires a designator such as `APT` or `STE` on mail for an address location that has one. Two units can share a street number and building point. An address without the unit may locate the building while omitting the detail needed on the mailpiece.

Mailwoman's [corpus address roles](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/v11.0.0/packages/corpus/lib/types.ts) distinguish a premise, mailing address, registered office, and other source claims. A registered office and a premises address can each be accurate records for one organization while serving different purposes. Their text or coordinates cannot establish whether they lead to the same entrance or mailbox.

## A mailing address need not locate the recipient's building

USPS [Publication 28](https://pe.usps.com/text/pub28/28c2_036.htm) standardizes a Post Office Box line as `PO BOX` followed by its identifier. At some locations, USPS also permits the [Post Office street address with a box designation](https://pe.usps.com/text/pub28/28c2_039.htm). That street-looking form still directs mail to a box at the Post Office.

For the synthetic input `PO Box 1234, Springfield, IL 62701`, published `mailwoman@11.0.0` returned kind `po_box` and a `po_box` node containing `PO Box 1234`. The parse identifies the form. It doesn't establish that the box exists or that the postal operator will deliver to it. The [PO box guide](../guides/handle-po-boxes.mdx) shows the result and a mailing-review policy.

## A postcode serves a postal system

Canada Post [defines](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/support/articles/addressing-guidelines/postal-codes.page) a six-character postcode in the `ANA NAN` form. Its first three characters identify a forward sortation area. The last three identify a local delivery unit. That unit can describe a city block, one building, a large-volume receiver, or a rural community. A postcode helps route mail. Its shape doesn't locate an individual building.

The published Mailwoman parse of bare `10118` matched the `us_zip`, `fr_postcode`, and `de_postcode` shapes. All three are format matches for the same five characters. The [bare-postcode guide](../guides/handle-bare-postcodes.mdx) keeps the country choice separate from this text check.

Mailwoman can label components and an application can check whether those components meet its stated policy. A geographic resolver can then identify a place when suitable data is installed. Delivery to a particular unit or box needs evidence from the postal operator. The [record-check guide](../guides/validate-records.mdx) shows an application policy, and [data and locales](../reference/data-and-locales.mdx) describes the geographic data needed for lookup.
