# What supports a model-quality claim?

A plausible parse of one address shows what Mailwoman returned for that input. It doesn't show how frequently the parser is right on addresses it hasn't learned from. A quality claim needs the training lineage, an independent evaluation set, and a comparison with the published model.

## Where the training examples came from

The [address-source register](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/main/packages/corpus/data/address-source-register.json) records publishers, source URLs, decisions about use, and terms. Corpus rows retain source identifiers, versions, licenses, and address roles. A row marked as a mailing address can be accurate source data without identifying a building point. Source lineage tells a reader what evidence the model learned from and which terms apply. It doesn't measure whether the trained model will parse a new address correctly.

The published `@mailwoman/neural-weights-en-us@11.0.0` [model card](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/v11.0.0/packages/neural-weights-en-us/model-card.json) identifies model `10.8.0` and corpus `v0.8.18-retention-overlay`. The [effective training manifest](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/v11.0.0/packages/corpus/data/training-manifests/v0.8.18-retention-overlay.effective.json) records the sources that reached the trainer. The package's [provenance record](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/v11.0.0/packages/neural-weights-en-us/PROVENANCE.json) connects the published weights to that corpus. These identifiers let a reviewer check whether a later evaluation overlaps the model's training inputs.

## Why evaluation addresses must be independent

If training and evaluation contain variants of the same address, the score can reward recognition of a familiar example. Mailwoman's [held-out specification](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/main/docs/engineering/HOLDOUT_AND_EVALUATION.mdx) groups related address rows, freezes evaluation collections, checks their labels, and audits overlap with the published and candidate training lineages. It distinguishes `verified_unseen`, `unseen_by_candidate`, `known_overlap`, and `unknown`. A paired comparison between models uses `verified_unseen` rows, where both models are being tested on addresses outside their training lineages.

The evaluation must also state which places, address forms, and sources its rows cover. A result for one country or form cannot establish a rate for another. A country with no admitted rows has unmeasured quality. A score of zero would require admitted rows and measured failures.

## What a release comparison adds

A candidate model is compared with the weights users can install, identified by the published `model.onnx` SHA-256 digest. The release procedure scores both models on the same frozen rows and reports the required country and capability outcomes, including measured losses. Its verdict applies to the candidate artifact and the registered release specification used for that read. The [model-work runbook](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/main/docs/engineering/CONTRIBUTING_MODEL_WORK.mdx) defines the procedure. Dated [evaluation records](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/tree/main/docs/records/evals) give the actual artifacts, rows, results, and verdicts.

The source register answers where training data came from. The held-out collection answers which independent addresses were tested. The paired release report answers how a particular candidate performed against a particular published model under stated requirements. These records measure parsing. Building existence, mail deliverability, and coordinate accuracy need other evidence. Geographic quality needs its own data identity and independently known positions. [Current product limits](/product/limits-and-status) separate published capabilities from release-matched evidence. [Confidence and ambiguity](./confidence-and-limits.mdx) explains why one node score cannot replace those evaluations.
