# What an address result can establish

Does a geocode result show that its coordinate is accurate and that its source may be used for a particular purpose? The result identifies what Mailwoman selected and can include a source stamp from the matched row. Accuracy requires an independent comparison. Permission depends on the dataset terms and the intended use.

## The result records a selected claim

A parsed `house_number` records text in the input. The geocoder's `resolution_tier` identifies the evidence used for its selected coordinate. The point may represent an address point, a street, a postcode, or a larger place. `epistemic_status` describes what the coordinate evidence supports. The tier and status answer different questions: how the point was produced and what claim its evidence permits. [Why a geocode coordinate has a precision tier](./geocoding.mdx) explains that selection.

For a stamped address-point or interpolation row, `coordinate_source` copies the row's `source` and, when present, `release`. A landmark result can record a venue source with `release: null`. Other paths can return `null`. The stamp reports what the selected row recorded. It doesn't reconstruct every upstream transformation. The [result schema](../reference/schema.mdx#geocode-result-and-provenance) defines when each field appears.

The published [bundle registry](https://github.com/sister-software/mailwoman/blob/v11.0.0/packages/mailwoman/lib/data/published/bundles.ts) lists source and rights records. An installed artifact's `layer_manifest` can record its source vintage, build identity, attribution, and license expression. The bundle declaration doesn't identify the file installed for a particular run. [Data and locales](../reference/data-and-locales.mdx) describes the installed data an operator needs to check.

## Rights and control require their own records

The source stamp doesn't grant permission to use the data. The [data and licensing page](/product/data-and-licensing) records publisher terms and unresolved questions for published bundles. A legal review must consider the exact artifact and intended use. A license record doesn't measure coordinate accuracy.

A data holder also decides whether its records may be retrieved, used for training, retained, or disclosed. An operator who runs Mailwoman in its own environment controls its deployment and local files. That deployment doesn't create permission from a contributor for every use of imported data. Those permissions require agreements and operating controls beyond the result fields.

## Accuracy requires an independent check

An accuracy claim needs an expected address or coordinate from an identified source, the exact input, the runtime and model versions, the selected data snapshot, the observed output, and an acceptance rule. The public docs have no release-matched geographic accuracy result for the published 11.0.0 bundles. A new data artifact is being built, so this page makes no claim about the coordinate returned for a particular address. [What supports a model-quality claim?](./data-and-model-quality.mdx) explains the separate evidence for parsing quality.

The [provenance review guide](../guides/review-geocode-evidence.mdx) shows how to inspect the result and associated terms. The [problem-report guide](../guides/report-a-problem.mdx) records the input, expected result, observed result, and first visible mismatch when an independent check fails.
