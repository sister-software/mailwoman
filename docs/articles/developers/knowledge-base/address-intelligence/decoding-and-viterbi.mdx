# Decoding and the best path

## The question

At the end of scoring, every piece of the input carries a number for each of the 33 available labels.
Taking each piece's highest-scoring label is the obvious next move, and it is wrong — it produces
sequences that are not readings of anything, like a "continues the locality" label with no locality
open in front of it. The standard symptom is `Saint Petersburg, FL` coming back with a locality of
`Petersburg`.

So who assembles the pieces, and on what basis?

## The analog

In a rule-based parser this is the reconciliation pass, and everyone who has written one has written
it the same way: run the classifiers, look at the mess, and clean it up with tie-breaks. Prefer the
longer span. If two rules claim the same token, prefer the more specific one. Drop a continuation
that has no beginning. If a state abbreviation and a street suffix both fire on `IN`, prefer the one
that keeps the rest of the parse coherent.

Those heuristics are doing something real, and they share a flaw: each one repairs a decision after
it was made, in isolation, and the repairs interact. Drop the orphaned continuation and you have
silently shortened a town name. Prefer the longer span and you have swallowed the house number.

The alternative is to stop repairing readings and start scoring them. Instead of picking each
piece's best label and fixing the result, ask which complete reading of the whole line scores highest
— where a reading that is not structurally possible scores nothing at all, so it is never in
contention and never needs repairing.

That is the difference between a route planner that picks the best next turn at every intersection
and one that scores whole routes. The turn-by-turn planner takes a fast road that dead-ends. The
route planner never proposes the dead end, because a route through it is not a route.

## Legality first

Before any search runs, the parser builds a table from the label list, once, that says which label
may follow which. The rule is short enough to state in full:

- Anything may be followed by `O`.
- Anything may be followed by a `B-` label, of any type. A new span can always start.
- `I-<type>` may follow only `B-<type>` or `I-<type>`. A continuation must continue something of the
  same kind.
- A sequence may not open on an `I-` label. There is nothing before the first piece to continue.

Everything the table permits scores zero, and everything it forbids scores as impossible. Notice what
the table does not contain: it holds no view about which permitted transition is likely. `B-region`
followed by `B-postcode` and `B-region` followed by `B-locality` are both legal, and the table ranks
them identically, even though real addresses use the first far more.

That is deliberate, and it is the answer to a question the label space raises. `I-locality` without a
`B-locality` is not an unlikely reading, it is not a reading. Encoding it as a hard constraint means
the orphan cannot appear in any candidate, so nothing downstream has to be defensive about it.

## Best path

With the table in hand, the decoder scores a whole reading as the sum of its per-piece scores plus
its transition scores, and searches for the highest-scoring legal reading of the entire line. The
search is the Viterbi algorithm, which is what makes this affordable: rather than enumerating every
combination, it sweeps left to right keeping, for each label at each position, the best way to reach
it, and then walks the backpointers home. Cost is proportional to the number of pieces times the
square of the label count, which for 24 pieces and 33 labels is small enough to disappear next to the
model itself.

Walk the failure it rules out. Suppose the model is unsure about `Saint` in `Saint Petersburg` —
scoring `O` a shade above `B-locality` — and confident about `Petersburg` as `I-locality`. Per-piece
argmax takes both winners and emits `O`, `I-locality`, which the span walk reads as a one-word town.
The best-path search never considers that pair, because the transition is impossible. Its best legal
option pays the small cost on `Saint` to take `B-locality` and keeps the large gain on `Petersburg`.
The model was not more sure; the reading was better.

Here is the part worth knowing about the shipped model, because it changes what the guarantee is
for. The classifier accepts a diagnostic per-piece decode alongside the default best-path one, so
the two can be compared directly. Run 56 varied inputs through both — lowercase, comma-free,
venue-led, fragments, non-Latin script, deliberately broken rows — and they produce byte-identical
output on every one. On this model, over that set, the constraint rescues nothing you can observe.

That is not an argument for removing it. A model well-fit enough to rarely emit an impossible
sequence is not a model that cannot, and the failure is silent when it happens: an orphan
continuation does not raise, it shortens a town name. The table costs one table build at startup and
a comparison per transition, and in exchange the tree that reaches your code is well-formed on input
nobody has tried yet. Guarantees are worth most exactly where measurement has nothing to report.

## Why the transition scores are constraints, not weights

A **conditional random field** is a sequence model whose decode step scores complete label sequences
using both per-item scores and learned pairwise scores between adjacent labels — the pairwise part
being where "region is followed by postcode more than by locality" would live, as a real number
learned from data.

Mailwoman decodes with Viterbi over a transition table, so the shape is a conditional random field's.
The transition table is not. It is built from the label list at startup, holds only legality, and is
never updated by training. Nothing in the shipped weights packages supplies a learned transition
matrix; the decoder has a slot for one and every published bundle leaves it empty. Training scores
each piece's label on its own — cross-entropy per position, with no sequence-level term — which is
what the model card records as training without a conditional random field and decoding with one.

One qualification, so the claim is not read wider than it is. A prior can contribute a bonus at a
specific transition at a specific position — evidence about one candidate span, supplied by a
gazetteer lookup and scoped to where it matched. What does not exist is a general preference between
two labels, learned from data and applied everywhere the pair occurs. That is the thing a learned
transition matrix would be, and it is the thing that does not ship.

The one-sentence history: a trainable transition matrix was built and trained jointly with the
per-piece loss, the two losses fought rather than cooperating, and after repeated attempts the
sequence-level term was set to zero and has stayed there. What survives is the part that was earning
its keep — the legality table — without the part that was not.

## What it costs

**The decoder has no view on what is common.** It rules out the impossible and is indifferent among
the possible, so a rare-but-legal ordering is never penalized and a frequent one is never rewarded.
All of that work sits in the model, whose scores are computed with the whole line visible rather than
piece by piece. The 56-input comparison above is what makes that arrangement look survivable: the
per-piece winners already formed a legal sequence on every one of those inputs, which is not what you
would see if the scores were being produced without reference to their neighbors.

**It cannot fix a confidently wrong label.** Best-path search redistributes among readings the scores
make close. When the model is sure and wrong, every legal reading agrees with it, and the decoder
returns the wrong answer in structurally valid form. Valid is not the same as correct — the tree that
comes out is always well-formed, and being well-formed is not evidence about the content.

**The guarantee stops at the decoder.** Span construction downstream is lenient by design: hand it an
invalid sequence and it will treat a hanging continuation as the start of a new span rather than
raising. That tolerance exists so a caller who runs the diagnostic per-piece decode still gets output,
and it means the structural guarantee is a property of the default decode path, not of the tree
builder.
