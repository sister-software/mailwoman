# Placetype evidence — the projection table and the census design

How the WOF placetype vocabulary meets the `ComponentTag` grammar, why the decoder takes _nudges_
from the hierarchy rather than constraints, and the design the pair-index arc is the first
production instance of. Codified 2026-08-01 from the hierarchy-evidence conversations (the
"Queens under New York" formulation) and the GB dependent-locality arc's receipts.

## The two ontologies

WOF placetypes (~33: `borough`, `microhood`, `dependency`, `concourse`, …) describe what a place
**is**. `ComponentTag` (see [SCHEMA](./SCHEMA.mdx)) describes what role a span plays in an
address's **grammar**. They are different vocabularies with a many-to-one **projection**, and the
projection — not the placetype — is what the decoder can use. SCHEMA.mdx already fixes the
canonical example: `borough` is not a tag; `dependent_locality` is the umbrella term borough
projects onto.

**The rule: no placetype gets its own tag; every placetype gets a projection, and hierarchy
evidence is computed over projections.**

## From pairs to the census

The shipped **placetype-pair index** (`pair-index-gb.bin`, `pair-index-nz.bin`) is the sharpest
instance of the general idea: known (child surface, parent surface) memberships, applied as a soft
logit bias at decode time. Its three-way ablation is the composition proof (0/69 and 3/69 for
model-alone and index-alone; 69/69 together).

The general form is the **placetype census**: per gazetteer node, the distribution of its
children's _projected_ tags ("this parent: 5 borough-children, 90 neighbourhoods, 0 dependent
localities"). That turns a globally-rare tag into a conditionally-common one exactly where it
should be — `dependent_locality`'s global prior is tiny, its prior _given this subtree_ is large.
The census is a data artifact in the same family as the pair index (the PIX1 tag byte already
anticipates multiple placetypes), shipped and versioned like the lexicons, probed at decode time.

## The doctrine constraints (standing, non-negotiable)

1. **Positive evidence only.** Presence boosts; absence stays neutral. A subtree with no boroughs
   must never _forbid_ borough — gazetteer absence is usually coverage, not fact (WOF Japan is
   point-geometry; the meaning-of-zero rule).
2. **Bias, never mask.** The model owns segmentation; the hierarchy contributes soft evidence.
   Hard matching is where venue-confound false positives live (window-mode measured 52% FP; gated
   segment mode is why the shipped floor is sub-2%).
3. **Per-locale conventions still gate expectations.** One model serves every locale; what a
   locale _expects_ to emit stays governed by its conventions (codex address-system conventions).
4. **Locale-scoped artifacts must be provably inert elsewhere.** en-us ships no pair index and a
   dedicated test asserts GB-shaped input takes NO pair bias through en-us — a packaging seatbelt,
   not a doctrine. The US joins the census program through its own instances (boroughs first).

## Why en-us has no pair index today (and why that's sequencing, not doctrine)

The GB/NZ indexes exist because those POSTAL formats carry the dependent-locality/suburb line and
national registers (HM Land Registry PPD, LINZ) provide millions of pair rows. The US format has
no such line — USPS routes city/state/ZIP — so there is no postal register to harvest and no mail
-correctness stake. But the schema's umbrella term means US dependent localities exist (NYC's five
boroughs), and WOF supplies the pairs directly (211 boroughs with parents, globally). The US
instance = those census entries + the conditional-bias mechanism; its gate is the tag's
_contextual_ aliveness for US (the same dead-tag-conditional problem GB solved), not data.

## Coverage boundaries measured so far (2026-08-01 probe set)

The GB pair source (PPD) is England+Wales and leaves CITY empty for London — so London
neighbourhoods, estates, Northern Ireland, and the Republic sit outside the shipped index by the
source's own construction. Extensions are data acquisitions in the proven shape (ONS/OS open data
for London neighbourhoods; NI and IE sources), not new mechanism.
