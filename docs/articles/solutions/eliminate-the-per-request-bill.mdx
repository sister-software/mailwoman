# Eliminate the per-request bill

Metered geocoding charges for each lookup, so the line item grows with address volume. Your team has
little control over that volume. A migration reprocesses the address book, a campaign launches, or an
integration retries a queue. To budget for the year, you have to forecast query counts twelve months
ahead and then defend a number that changes for reasons outside your control.

## What changes

Mailwoman runs where your code runs. Nothing meters usage, because the software executes on your
machines and sends no requests that anyone could count. The AGPL-3.0 release is free at any volume.
The commercial license releases you from the AGPL's source-sharing condition. It costs a flat $250 a
month per legal entity for a company under 250 employees and under $10M in annual revenue. A company
above either limit uses the Enterprise tier, which is scoped with you and starts at $15,000 a year.
Both tiers are flat, and your rate is fixed at signup.

Disk space replaces the meter. A full install of the entry package with the `en-us` weights measures
707 MB, of which 523 MB is the ONNX runtime. The global candidate gazetteer is a 1.65 GB download
that you pull once and keep. Both figures are measured.
[Footprints](../developers/reference/footprints.mdx) prints the commands that produced them, so you
can re-run them yourself.

## A book that grows

Consider a fulfillment back office that geocodes 500,000 addresses a month and grows to 5 million a
month eighteen months later. Providers publish their own rates, and this page quotes none of them.
At an illustrative $1 per 1,000 lookups, the metered cost grows from $500 a month to $5,000 a month.
Within the size limits above, the commercial license costs $250 a month at 500,000 addresses and $250
a month at 5 million, because the deployment reports no counts back to us. Growth raises the metered
cost and leaves the license cost unchanged.

## What you still carry

The price covers the code. Reference data has its own terms. The gazetteer and layer artifacts come
from open sources, and some of their terms follow the data into your product.
[Security and compliance](../about/security-and-compliance.mdx) explains where those terms apply and
identifies the two cases to read before a review. You also carry the operations: a process to run, a
disk to fill, and a decision about when to refresh. Those costs do not grow with address volume,
unlike a metered bill.
